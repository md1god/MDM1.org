Harvest — Complete Project Plan

Turn your farm images into smart decisions in minutes.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, distribution plan, and risks. The project is under development and has not been launched yet.

Product Plan: A machine learning platform for agriculture

1) Target Audience

• Researchers and graduate students in plant sciences and agriculture (top priority).

• Small and medium-sized AgTech companies building computer vision systems (pest classification, yield estimation, vehicle vision).

• Farmers and cooperatives who need ready-to-use models on their phones without any programming experience.

• Volunteer developers and environmental associations looking to build aerial image analysis tools.

• Government institutions and agricultural agencies that need automated crop reports.

2) Key Differentiators (Why Choose Our Product)

• No installation required: runs entirely in the browser using WASM and WebGPU, with a cloud option when needed.

• A structured agricultural task library in Arabic and English: pests, weeds, flowering, fruit size, yield estimation, heatmap — with a unified data card for each task.

• Standardized benchmarking: a unified leaderboard with a pre-written evaluation protocol to prevent "cherry-picking the best result after testing".

• Advanced synthetic data generator: blends real field images with simulated lighting, soil, and plant appearance to generate training data when labeled data is scarce.

• Runs on low-end devices (Edge): export models to ONNX/TFLite with a compression path tailored for field devices.

• Ethical data foundation: every dataset is clearly licensed, with a source record and consent, and a channel for farmers to upload their images with attribution.

• Ready-made analytics dashboards: PDF reports and interactive charts that non-technical management can understand.

3) Scope of the First Release (Maximum 8 Tasks)

1. User account creation with private workspaces and team invitations.

2. Central data browser: search and filter by task, crop, language, and license.

3. One-click model training: select task → data → model → train, with a live learning curve.

4. Direct inference by image upload: display results as heatmaps and detection boxes with confidence scores.

5. Synthetic data generator: automatically generate labeled images with varying lighting, angles, and noise.

6. Benchmark comparison dashboard: a standardized result table for each task with reproducibility.

7. Edge export: ONNX/TFLite with a model compression guide for field devices.

8. Farmer-facing app: test models on mobile and record notes with no programming required.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free-to-start: $0 — includes data browser, 3 trainings per month up to 5,000 images, inference up to 200 images/month, and basic export.

• Pro: $29/month per user — unlimited trainings, synthetic data generator, PDF reports, priority processing, and full edge export.

• Team: $99/month for up to 5 members — shared workspace, permissions, private benchmarking, and dedicated support.

• Enterprise: usage-based pricing — private hosting, API integration, compliance commitments, and on-field training.

Additional revenue sources: 15% commission on the ready-made models and datasets marketplace (split between the platform and the owner), custom fine-tuning and training services, and sponsorships for community academies.

5) Global Distribution Plan via Free Channels

1. Ongoing Arabic and English content: 3 videos per week titled "How to classify a pest in 5 minutes" on YouTube, LinkedIn, and X.

2. Free university workshops: direct outreach to agriculture and computer science departments to showcase tools and connect with researchers.

3. Researcher ambassador program: 100 researchers across 10 countries receive Pro for free in exchange for content and field testing.

4. Open-source repositories: publish parts of the processing tools and documentation on GitHub with open documentation and a Discord community channel.

5. Engagement in digital farming communities: outreach through WhatsApp, Facebook, and agricultural e-commerce groups with practical, non-technical offerings.

6. Model-building competitions: 3 annual competitions to convert real field images into open datasets, with symbolic prizes.

7. Visibility in agri-tech news outlets, podcasts, and short recorded webinars.

8. Free practical partnerships: integration with open-source drone analytics tools and government agricultural data centers.
