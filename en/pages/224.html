صنف — Complete Project Plan

Know what's in your food with a single tap, and help build the largest open food database for the Arab world.

Complete implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, target audience, product, business model, growth plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Health-conscious consumers: Those who read ingredients and want to know sugar, salt, fat, and additives before purchasing.

• Those with allergies and special diets: People with diabetes, gluten sensitivity, vegetarians, and those following a specific diet.

• Developers and product teams: Those building fitness, nutrition, or e-commerce apps who need structured food data via API.

• Nutrition researchers and students: Those who need large, queryable, and analyzable datasets.

• Volunteer contributors: Product photographers, open data activists, and category editors.

2) Key Differentiators (Why choose our product)

• Start with Arabic and the region first: Cover local products (regional brands not currently covered) with an authentic Arabic interface and layout, then expand globally.

• Quality over quantity: An automated verification pipeline (barcode image fingerprint + ingredient conflict checks) gives each product card a visible confidence score.

• Modern design from scratch: Simple and fast user experience (web app first, works without installation) instead of outdated, cluttered interfaces.

• Truly open data: Explicit open license for data + free API at the start, with no restrictive contractual terms.

• Contribute in one step: Scanning a single barcode is enough to add a product, with reputation rewards for contributors.

3) Scope of the First Version (max 8 tasks)

1. Search by name/barcode and a product page displaying ingredients and nutrition facts.

2. Add a product by photos (barcode + front image + nutrition table) with basic verification.

3. Automatic quality classification (sugar/salt/saturated fat/additives) with a simple explanation.

4. Smart filters based on allergies and dietary preferences (gluten, diabetes, vegetarian, etc.).

5. Public read-only API (product and search endpoints) with free API keys.

6. Simple contributor dashboard: my contributions, reputation, pending edits.

7. Bilingual support (Arabic and English) with a structure that allows adding more languages.

8. Open data export (CSV/JSON with one click) and periodic data dumps.

4) Pricing and Business Model

• Free at the start: Consumer use, contributions, and API up to a generous limit (e.g. 1,000 requests/day) — no intrusive ads.

• Pro plan for developers: Higher request limits, additional data (edit history, batches), and support — via a tiered monthly subscription.

• Enterprise: Custom data licenses, SLA, and integration with retailer and health systems.

• Community funding: Donations, institutional sponsors for open data, and research grants.

• Paid services: Cleaning and standardizing company product lists for a fee (without withholding basic data).

5) Global Growth Plan Through Free Channels

• Short-form video content: \"What's in this product?\" clips on short video platforms for awareness.

• Open data communities: Publish in open source forums, Reddit, and developer groups.

• Free integrations: Open-source templates that use the API to quickly build health apps.

• Universities and health associations: Research partnerships and student projects to verify data.

• Multilingual SEO: Indexed product pages that display nutrition facts in search results.

• Contribution challenge campaigns: Weekly challenges to photograph local products with a reputation leaderboard.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legal: Licenses, product images, and trademarks — addressed through explicit open data licensing, image rights review, and respecting trademarks without making ownership claims.

• Privacy: Contributor data — apply data minimization and a clear deletion policy (GDPR-compliant).

• Health liability: Display facts, not medical advice, with a disclaimer clarifying that scores are indicative.

• Technical: Contributor data quality and misuse — addressed with automated verification, human review, and a reputation system.

• Sustainability: Data growth increases costs — addressed with efficient search indexes, low-cost storage, and caching.

• Market dependency: Competition from existing databases — addressed by focusing on Arabic regional coverage and a modern interface.

7) Proposed Name and Tagline

Proposed name: صنف

Tagline: Know what's in your food with a single tap, and help build the largest open food database for the Arab world.
