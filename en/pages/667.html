Nasaq — Complete Project Plan

Plan for cross-sector energy from your open data to an actionable decision.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

1) Target audience

• Researchers and engineering and energy students at universities.

• Electricity companies, utilities, and renewable energy project developers.

• Ministries of energy and environment and regulatory bodies.

• Consulting firms and urban planning offices.

• Non-governmental organizations and development finance institutions.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Cross-sector modeling that links electricity, heat, hydrogen, transport, and industry in one engine.

• Runs in the browser without setting up a complex software environment, with the option to use it locally.

• Ready-to-edit data for different countries and regions, with CSV, Excel, and API imports.

• Full transparency: every assumption and result is traceable and reviewable, with export in open formats.

• Instant scenario comparison instead of running separate models and manually merging results.

• Designed for developing contexts: Arabic, English, and French interfaces, with less complex data and faster processing.

• Free to start with an open-source core, so no lock-in and no upfront costs.

3) First version scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Create a project and import demand and technology data from CSV and Excel.

2. A ready library of sector technologies: electricity, heat, hydrogen, transport, industry.

3. Build a simplified network with hourly time constraints and allocation of generation and storage capacities.

4. Run cost and emissions optimization through an open engine with input validation.

5. Create multiple scenarios and compare results in one dashboard.

6. Display cost, emissions, energy mix, and reliability indicators.

7. Export PDF/Excel reports and reusable results.

8. Share the project via a read-only link and basic collaboration between users.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever: open-source core + a free cloud account for one project and a limited number of scenarios.

• Pro: about $29 per user per month; more projects and scenarios, faster computing, and advanced reports.

• Team: about $99 per team per month; collaboration, permissions, and shared scenario comparison.

• Enterprise: custom pricing for private deployment, training, support, and system integration.

• Additional revenue: data hosting, training courses, technical support, and custom modeling services.

• No credit card required to start, with discounts for universities and non-profit organizations.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Publish the open repository, documentation, and examples on code hosting platforms.

• Short video tutorials on YouTube and technical posts on LinkedIn.

• Partnerships with universities through graduation projects and free workshops.

• Hackathons and energy modeling competitions with symbolic prizes.

• An open discussion community and a Q&A forum.

• Search engine optimization through guides and articles on energy planning.

• Monthly webinars with experts and users to present practical cases.

• A student ambassador program in different regions to promote the product locally.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: open data licenses and the need for attribution; data privacy in collaborative projects; a clear disclaimer that results are indicative and not a binding decision; compliance with third-party library licenses.

• Technical: slow optimization with large networks; incomplete or inconsistent data quality; difficulty verifying results; cloud computing costs; account and project security; maintaining reproducibility of results with engine updates.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Nasaq (نَسَق)

Tagline: Plan for cross-sector energy from your open data to an actionable decision.
