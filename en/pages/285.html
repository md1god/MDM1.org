Rasd — Complete Project Plan

What you see today becomes tomorrow's map.

A complete plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, rollout, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

An original digital product plan in field surveys and mapping

1) Target audience

• Everyday user: someone who notices daily problems in their city (broken streetlight, missing sign, blocked path) and wants to document them fast.

• Environmental and civic volunteers: local associations and initiatives needing a simple field tool for organized reporting.

• Researchers and students: anyone collecting recurring field data (commercial survey, geography study) at the lowest cost.

• Municipalities and local bodies: a channel for documented reports with precise coordinates, without an expensive system.

• Core segments: residents of mid-sized cities in emerging countries, where free open platforms spread well.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Offline-first: the app works fully without connection, syncing later — essential for field work.

• One-tap quick tasks: instead of long forms, short "check cards" done in seconds.

• Local suggestion intelligence: tasks suggested by your location, preference history, and season.

• Open architecture: API and data format documented, with open export in common formats.

• Optional advanced editor: a professional mode for detailed edits, serving beginner and expert alike.

• Light social ranking: trust points and badges keep volunteers going, without complexity.

• Lighter and faster: small (Lite) app size for budget devices and weak networks.

3) Scope of the first release (8 tasks maximum)

1. Base map with location and nearby tasks shown.

2. Three ready-made check cards only (no open forms).

3. Report submission with coordinates, optional photo, short comment.

4. Offline mode with an automatic sync queue.

5. My reports log with accepted/rejected status.

6. Simple points and badges system for volunteers.

7. Sign-in by email or as a guest, linked later.

8. Export my data in CSV.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: basic surveying and reporting functions free for individuals and volunteers.

• Organizations plan (SaaS): monthly subscription for municipalities and associations: report dashboard, analytics, team management, priority support.

• Partner API: pricing by request volume for those integrating the data into their systems.

• Initiative sponsorship: a thank-you for funders of local community initiatives (transparent, no intrusive ads).

• Goal: recurring revenue from organizations covering platform development, while individual use stays free forever.

5) Global rollout plan using free channels

• App store and SEO: multi-language store page with long-tail keywords.

• Communities: Reddit, Mastodon, Telegram, Discord, and local volunteer groups.

• Partnerships: university geography departments, hobby clubs, environmental associations, "cleaner my city" drives.

• Content: short clips explaining one task in 15 seconds, user-generated.

• Events: seasonal documentation races (Challenge) with badges and symbolic prizes.

• Tech and local media: product stories sent to blogs and free newsletters.

• Community translation: volunteer translators for the most spoken languages.

• Viral growth: a direct invite link to a task, plus a share button for the problem.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

Legal:

• Photo and location privacy: images may show people or shopfronts — face blurring and explicit consent required.

• Licensing: all content released under a clear open license, checked against data linked to restricted-license sources.

• Local aspects: some countries ban photographing certain facilities or sharing precise coordinates.

• Liability: an explicit notice that data is user reports, with no guarantee of accuracy.

Technical:

• Misuse or spam of reports by competitors or bots — simple verification, rate limiting, anomaly detection.

• Hosting cost of maps and images at scale — image compression and tiered storage.

• Sync conflicts from simultaneous edits — timestamps and a simple conflict resolution.

• Dependence on an external source for base maps — support for more than one source plus a backup.

• Uneven spread across languages and regions — launching with only three languages in the first release.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: Rasd

Marketing tagline: What you see today becomes tomorrow's map.
