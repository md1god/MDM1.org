GridWeave — Complete Project Plan

A cloud platform that models power grids and simulates and optimizes renewable energy scenarios in the browser, free to start.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, growth plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

An original product plan for power grid analysis and energy planning

1) Target audience

• Grid engineers and planners at electricity companies and transmission and distribution operators who conduct expansion and interconnection studies.

• Researchers and graduate students in energy engineering and systems who need reproducible simulation tools.

• Renewable energy companies and consultants who assess project feasibility and integration scenarios.

• Regulators and policymakers who examine the impact of legislation and carbon-neutrality pathways.

• Developers and data scientists working on climate, demand, and generation data.

2) Points of distinction (why choose our product)

• Runs in the browser with no software environment setup: instant start versus libraries that require installation and complex configuration.

• A visual drag-and-drop interface + Python SDK + REST API: speaks to both the engineer and the developer in a single product.

• An intelligent assistant that converts a text description or CSV files into a ready grid model with error detection and correction.

• An optimized cloud engine that runs capacity expansion planning and AC/DC load flow without DevOps overhead or buying expensive solver licenses.

• Real-time collaboration and exportable, comparable scenarios with full transparency in results and assumptions.

• Public data sources connected automatically: climate, demand, topology, and fuel prices — all in open formats.

• A generous free tier and an open-source core that builds trust and community growth instead of a closed commercial lock-in.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. A visual drag-and-drop grid editor (buses, lines, generators, loads, storage).

2. An AC/DC load flow engine with basic stability analysis.

3. Renewable energy and storage modeling via time series (hourly and quarter-hourly).

4. A capacity expansion planning module and comparison of build-out scenarios.

5. An intelligent assistant to convert a text description or CSV into a runnable model.

6. A Python SDK and REST API to run jobs and connect external systems.

7. Import/export in open formats (CSV/NetCDF) and connectors for public climate and demand data.

8. An interactive results dashboard + sharing links and team permission roles.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever (Free): public models, limited compute hours, one user, community support, open-source core.

• Pro (per seat/monthly): private non-public models, more compute hours, unlimited scenarios, extended export.

• Team: shared workspaces, permission control, version history, priority support.

• Enterprise: private or on-premises deployment, SSO, service level agreement, custom integration, training.

• Additional revenue: an add-on store (data connectors, solvers, regional templates), and consulting and training services.

• Strategy: an open-core model keeps the entry threshold free and attractive, and the subscription is paid for by cloud and collaborative value rather than hiding core features.

5) Global growth plan with free channels

• Open-source core on GitHub + a PyPI package to attract developers and researchers.

• University and energy lab partnerships with free academic licenses and ready-made curriculum templates.

• Educational content: interactive documentation, Jupyter notebooks, and YouTube tutorials and SEO blogs in multiple languages.

• Communities: a Discord/Matrix server, a support forum, and a student ambassador program.

• Hackathons and energy data challenges with small prizes to generate open models and projects.

• Free workshops at energy and climate conferences and attendance at open-source conferences.

• Galleries of ready-made templates shared publicly (reference grids) to attract users through search engines.

• Integration with popular tools (notebooks, data management systems) as a free acquisition channel.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: conflicting licenses of embedded libraries — we rely on a core written from scratch with a compatibility review of every dependency. The question of ownership of imported data and its licensing. Patent risks in optimization algorithms — we prefer published and open methods. Compliance with data protection laws and sanctions when serving customers in restricted countries.
