Extora — Complete Project Plan

Build browser extensions and publish them with one click.

A full execution plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, launch plan, and risks. Under construction, not yet launched.

Product plan: a platform for building and publishing browser extensions

1) Target audience

• Independent developers and small teams building browser extensions quickly.

• Entrepreneurs testing product ideas (SaaS) through extensions before building full apps.

• Companies that need internal tools running inside their employees' browsers.

• Beginner developers who want an interface more abstract than configuring the manifest manually.

• Digital agencies building custom extensions for their clients.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• A cloud-based platform with a live preview of the extension while editing in the browser.

• One-click publishing to Chrome Web Store, Firefox Add-ons, and Edge Add-ons via OAuth.

• Manifest V3-first architecture with automatic file updates when store policies change.

• Ready templates (popup, options page, content script, background service worker) creating a ready project in seconds.

• A simplified messaging system between extension components without manual wiring.

• A built-in dashboard to measure installs, crash reporting, and A/B testing.

• A permanent free tier to keep the community and credibility (free-to-start model).

3) First version scope (8 tasks maximum)

1. Create a new project with an automatic manifest.json file per MV3.

2. Live preview of the extension while editing (popup and content script) in the browser.

3. Four ready basic templates: popup, options, content script, service worker.

4. A unified messaging layer between background, content scripts, and popup.

5. A build producing a .crx and .xpi file ready for manual upload.

6. OAuth connection to developer accounts on the browser stores for direct publishing.

7. A basic analytics dashboard: installs, errors, response time.

8. Concise documentation in Arabic and English with ready-to-copy examples.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: one project, basic templates, local build, manual publishing, community support.

• Pro — 19$/month: unlimited projects, automatic publishing, advanced analytics, A/B tests.

• Team — 49$/month: shared workspaces, role permissions, CI/CD integration.

• Template marketplace: selling ready templates/extensions — 25% commission (70% to the developer, 5% processing).

• The permanent free model as an acquisition funnel toward the paid plans.

5) Global launch plan using free channels

• Multilingual educational content on YouTube, X (Twitter), and LinkedIn in Arabic, English, Spanish, and Japanese.

• Developer communities: sharing on Reddit (r/browserextensions, r/webdev), Hacker News, Dev.to, Hashnode.

• Launches on product launch platforms: Product Hunt, Uneed, BetaList.

• Tech creator partnerships: cross-promotions with creators who have a matching audience.

• SEO-driven blog: comparison articles, explanations of common MV3 problems, and user success stories.

• An active community: Discord server and Telegram channel for support and contributions (open-source snippets).

• Virtual hackathons: monthly extension-building challenges with free Pro subscription prizes.

• Referral system: a user invites a user = a free Pro month for both.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal — store policies: policy changes could disrupt automatic publishing; solution: continuous monitoring of policies and APIs, and compliance with each store's terms.

• Legal — privacy: usage analytics fall under GDPR/CCPA; solution: anonymization, explicit consent, and a clear privacy policy.

• Legal — trademarks: avoid logos or names of Chrome/Firefox/Edge without official permission.

• Technical — MV3 evolution: ongoing Manifest V3 updates may require restructuring; solution: an abstraction layer over the APIs plus periodic updates.

• Technical — security: a platform flaw could endanger millions of users; solution: periodic security audits, sandboxing, and strict permission limits.

• Technical — competition: Google or Microsoft could ship similar built-in tools; solution: differentiation via automatic publishing, analytics, and multi-store support.

• Technical — OAuth dependency: sudden store API changes could break the integration; solution: an abstraction layer and alternative providers.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: Extora

Marketing tagline: Build browser extensions and publish them with one click.
