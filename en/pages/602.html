Wusul — Complete Project Plan

Audit your site the way AI agents read it and get ready-made fixes before you lose customers.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target audience

• SaaS teams and startups that rely on their website to attract customers or sell subscriptions.

• E-commerce stores that want shopping agents to read their products and prices correctly.

• SEO and digital marketing agencies managing multiple client websites.

• Web developers and product teams responsible for technical architecture and rendering.

• Publishers and content sites that fear losing visibility to agents.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• It measures what the agent actually does when fetching and reading, not what a traditional SEO audit assumes.

• It provides reproducible evidence: the request, the response, the reason for failure, and the suggested fix.

• Outputs ready for both machines and humans: JSON, Markdown, and copyable fix files.

• Focus on practical priority: what first prevents the agent from understanding the offer, price, or product.

• Starts free and instantly with no signup, then scales to continuous monitoring and teams.

• Transparent methodology and scores, with the ability to re-scan and compare results.

• Bilingual support (Arabic and English) and measuring agent readiness in emerging markets.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Scan a single URL and detect bot blocking, robots.txt, and access policies.

2. Compare static HTML with content after JavaScript execution to detect pages that are empty for agents.

3. Test extraction of price, product, and offer from sensitive pages such as pricing and product pages.

4. Detect the presence of llms.txt, sitemap.xml, OpenAPI, MCP, and Schema.org.

5. Issue a unified readiness score with a clear reason for each success or failure.

6. Generate a shareable report in JSON and Markdown with copyable fixes.

7. Quick re-scan and compare the score before and after the fix.

8. A simple dashboard to save recent scans without complexity or early integrations.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: limited instant scans per day, basic report, no credit card.

• Pro: low monthly subscription for entrepreneurs and individual developers, including deeper scans, history, and comparisons.

• Team: multiple seats, saved projects, email alerts, and exportable reports.

• Agency: larger number of sites, white-label reports, and scheduled scanning for multiple clients.

• API usage: usage-based pricing for enterprises and CI/CD tools.

• Paid add-ons: continuous monitoring, change alerts, and optional manual review.

• No selling customer data, and annual plans with a clear discount.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Free programmatic content pages for each framework and deployment platform explaining readiness fixes.

• An open-source CLI tool and contributable scanning rules on GitHub.

• Launch on Product Hunt, Hacker News, developer communities, and SEO.

• A free embeddable readiness badge for websites that creates organic spread.

• Educational comparison articles between what the user sees and what the agent sees.

• Free integrations with popular hosting platforms and CI/CD tools.

• An Arabic and English version with community workshops and collaboration with universities and tech communities.

• An ambassador and agency program offering limited free scans in exchange for educational coverage.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: respect robots.txt and terms of use, do not bypass firewalls or paywalls, and do not misleadingly impersonate a commercial agent.

• Legal: comply with GDPR and CCPA, minimize data collection, document retention periods, and provide a data processing agreement for teams.

• Legal: avoid claims guaranteeing visibility within specific systems, and clarify that results are estimates.

• Technical: volatility of agent behavior and changing User-Agents, requiring constant rule updates.

• Technical: the cost of JavaScript execution and slow scanning, with caching and rate limits.

• Technical: false positives and negatives, with evidence explanation and a confidence score for each result.

• Technical: protect the scanner from SSRF and abuse of scanning third-party domains, with allowlists and strict limits.

• Technical: key and shared report security, with temporary permissions, encryption, and redaction of sensitive data.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Wusul (وُصول)

Tagline: Aud
