NAWA — Complete Project Plan

From a single cell to a vibrant world — learn evolution while you play.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, distribution plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

Product plan: a life-evolution simulation game (from a cell to a complex organism)

1) Target audience

• Middle and high school biology students (ages 12–18).

• Science teachers looking for an interactive classroom educational tool.

• Fans of scientific and strategy games on PC and browser.

• Educational content creators and simplifiers for biology.

• Specifically the Arab audience with full Arabic language support (a major gap in this genre).

2) Points of distinction (why our product is chosen)

• Runs directly in the browser (WebGL / WebGPU) with no download or installation.

• Full native Arabic support and a right-to-left interface from day one.

• Real, open, interpretable evolution algorithms (mutations, selection, environmental pressure).

• An educational path tied to curriculum concepts with progress reports for the teacher.

• A lightweight experience that runs on mid-range devices, unlike heavy simulators.

• Simplified 3D construction with a fully original art style (no transferred assets).

• Collaborative sessions and classroom competitions.

• A free model from the start that lowers the entry barrier.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Build the first cell simulation engine (metabolism, energy, movement, division).

2. Mutation and natural selection system with environmental variables.

3. Organism editor to customize form and biological functions.

4. Multiple environments (ocean, light, heat, resources) with difficulty levels.

5. A native user interface in Arabic and English with full control.

6. Save progress and share the evolved organism via a link.

7. An educational progress dashboard for the teacher with measurable learning objectives.

8. Performance and compatibility optimization for browsers and mobile devices.

4) Pricing and profit model

• Free-to-start: basic single-player play and the organism editor are fully free.

• Optional membership (monthly/annual subscription): visual appearances, special strains, advanced cloud saving, deeper statistics.

• Educational license for schools: teacher dashboard, multiple classes, group reports.

• Cosmetic DLC packs that do not affect scientific balance.

• Sponsorship/grants from educational institutions to support free access for public schools.

5) Global distribution plan using free channels

• Video publishing on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Reels with impressive “from cell to organism” clips.

• Reddit and Discord communities specialized in games, simulation, and biology.

• Collaboration with biology teachers and educational content creators in exchange for free access.

• Open educational resource platforms and teacher groups on Facebook.

• Release on itch.io and GitHub Pages, and explaining the technology on programming forums.

• SEO content with educational articles in Arabic that attract students and teachers.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: avoid any assets, code, or names from any existing product; write everything from scratch and review licenses before including any library.

• Legal: respect the rights of music, sounds, and images through licensed or internally created assets.

• Technical: large-scale simulation performance in the browser → solutions: simplifying models, WebGPU techniques, and progressive updates.

• Technical: cross-browser and cross-device compatibility → automated testing and rendering fallbacks.

• Technical: scalability for group play → lightweight service architecture and caching.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: NAWA (نَواة)

Marketing tagline: From a single cell to a vibrant world — learn evolution while you play.
