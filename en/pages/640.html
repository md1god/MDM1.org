Sarayan — Complete Project Plan

Fast, reliable fluid simulation on any device, from your laptop to your computing cluster.

Complete implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction, not yet launched.

Product plan: fast computational fluid simulation on any hardware

1) Target audience

• Fluid and aerodynamics engineers at SMEs needing fast simulation without costly licenses.

• Researchers and graduate students in physics, mechanical and environmental engineering.

• Simulation and scientific game developers who want a fast, embeddable library.

• Industrial design teams needing instant visual assessment of air or water flow.

• Educational institutions seeking a tool for old or new lab computers.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Local-first: no cloud or specific hardware, with automatic acceleration via WebGPU, Vulkan/Metal, CUDA, and CPU fallback.

• Ten times faster setup: import geometry, choose a fluid, run in under two minutes.

• Modern visual interface with parameter editor and live flow view, not complex configuration files.

• Performance transparency: built-in metrics compare accuracy and speed on the user's hardware, with published validation.

• Scale without code changes: from laptop to cluster via command-line interface and Python API.

• Free tier from the start covering individual local use fully, with no time limit or watermark.

• Data privacy: simulations and geometries stay on the user's device by default.

3) First-version scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Core 2D/3D LBM engine (D2Q9 and D3Q19) for incompressible flows with simple boundaries.

2. Multi-hardware execution layer: WebGPU for web, Vulkan/Metal/CUDA for desktop, CPU SIMD fallback.

3. Geometry import from STL/OBJ, with ready-made scene generator and boundary setup.

4. Visual interface for velocity, viscosity, mesh, and time settings, with live preview.

5. Validation suite of 10 reference cases with reproducible accuracy and performance report.

6. Export to VTK, CSV, images, and short video.

7. Command-line interface and Python API for batch runs and experiment automation.

8. Share results via lightweight link or local project file, without data upload by default.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever: individual local use, all core simulation features, unlimited export.

• Pro for individuals: $9/month or $79/year, with collaboration, advanced comparisons, and priority support.

• Team: $29/user/month, with shared workspaces and version history.

• Enterprise: annual on-premise license, custom integration support, no data upload.

• Optional usage-based cloud: billed only for compute minutes beyond local capacity.

• Additional revenue: ready-made templates, training courses, and commercial licensing of the embedding library.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• GitHub: examples, templates, and validation suite, with regular free releases.

• YouTube: short "From geometry to result in two minutes" series in multiple languages.

• Public leaderboard: transparent performance comparisons on users' hardware, driving organic sharing.

• Scientific communities: Reddit, Discord, LinkedIn, and CFD, with educational not promotional content.

• Universities: free educational licenses and hands-on workshops to build early adoption.

• SEO and technical content: articles on validating results and reducing memory use.

• Developer packages: Python library on PyPI and examples on Hugging Face and Colab.

• Hackathons and competitions with small prizes to attract contributions and examples.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: possible claims over published LBM algorithms or old patents; addressed by legal review, documented open research, and avoiding code or assets from other products.

• Licensing: risk of incompatible licenses contaminating the project; addressed with a clear dependency approval policy and automated scanning.

• Export and data: restrictions on simulation use in sensitive sectors; addressed with terms of use and clear classification.

• Technical: floating-point precision differences across devices; addressed with mandatory validation tests and documented backend limits.

• Unexpected performance: GPU driver variability; addressed with automatic fallback and built-in metrics.

• Result quality: user trust risks; addressed with published reference cases and comparisons to analytical solutions.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Sarayan

Tagline: Fast, reliable fluid simulation on any device, from your laptop to your computing cluster.
