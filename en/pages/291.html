صفّh — Full Project Plan

Test your offer on a thousand virtual buyers before spending a single riyal.

A full execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the go-to-market plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: the first offer-testing dashboard before launch

1) Target audience

• Primary: founders and product teams at early-stage startups (Seed–Series A) before spending any ad budget.

• Secondary: marketing and growth agencies running their clients' campaigns and looking to justify pricing and packaging decisions.

• Tertiary: product managers at mid-sized companies launching a new feature or package.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• A buyer-simulation model built on real behavioral segments (professional role, price sensitivity, past purchase decisions) rather than mere virtual people.

• Actionable outputs: a unified preference score for each offer + a ranked objections list with a suggested text edit for each objection.

• Cross-variable testing: ad × landing page × price × package, with a direct and clear comparison.

• Results within hours, using little compute per test.

3) First-version scope (8 tasks max)

1. Create a test project and upload the offer (ad copy + landing page description).

2. Choose the virtual buyers' segment (sample size, industry, region).

3. Run one simulation round and show the overall result for each offer.

4. Report the most frequent objections with the reason for each objection.

5. Compare two versions of the offer (A/B) on a single screen.

6. Test one pricing model (price tier × package elements).

7. Export a PDF/Markdown report ready to share with the team.

8. A simple sync dashboard showing round dates and preference changes.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: 3 simulations per month with a limited sample size (e.g., 200 virtual buyers) and a basic report.

• Pro — monthly: relatively unlimited simulations, A/B comparisons, full export, advanced segments.

• Team: multiple seats, a saved offers library, and an API for connecting to marketing tools.

• Additional revenue: optional "human review" packages where an expert reviews the simulation results and suggests alternative copy.

5) Global go-to-market plan using free channels

• Quality content: open reports titled "What did the virtual buyers reject this month?" published on the blog and X/LinkedIn.

• Participation in founder communities (Product Hunt alternatives communities, Indie Hackers, Hacker News Show HN).

• Ready-made templates: a landing page per industry (SaaS, apps, e-commerce) free to copy.

• Partnerships with small influencers in exchange for free usage credits.

• Weekly email series: a common pricing mistake + how the simulation exposed it.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: the results could be misread as a "verified market study"; it must be clear that this is a probabilistic simulation and not a human survey, with compliance with data-protection rules (GDPR) and no storage of real customer data.

• Legal also: avoid any claim guaranteeing sales results, and set terms of use that prevent using the platform to test fraudulent or misleading offers.

• Technical: simulation quality depends on language models; cultural biases may appear, so we need benchmark evaluation sets per market and continuous bias fixing.

7) Proposed name and tagline

The proposed name: صفّh

The tagline: Test your offer on a thousand virtual buyers before spending a single riyal.
