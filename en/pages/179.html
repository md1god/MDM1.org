Voltara — Complete Project Plan

An open energy platform that connects your devices, understands your consumption, and cuts your bill — start for free.

A complete execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, rollout plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: an Energy Internet of Things (Energy IoT) platform

1) Target audience

• Small and medium buildings: hotels, clinics, offices, retail chains that want to cut their electricity bill without a technical team.

• Light factories and production workshops: need load and meter monitoring and fault alerts.

• Solar and battery operators: tracking production, consumption, and charging.

• Developers and System Integrators: building energy apps on top of open APIs.

• The tech-savvy smart-home consumer: local control without forced cloud.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• A truly free start: a free cloud tier up to a certain device/data limit, with no credit card.

• Local-first: run the engine on a small on-site device, so sensitive data stays local and the system works when the internet goes down.

• Energy-native: the model is built from the ground up around meters, loads, prices, and grid carbon, not just generic channels.

• Multi-protocol ready: MQTT, Modbus, and connection to common gateways without writing code.

• Visual rules engine + intelligence: "if load exceeds X, disconnect/alert/reschedule," then automatic savings suggestions.

• Source transparency and export: no data lock-in, and you can export your rules and data at any time.

• Multi-tenant from the start to serve integrators and distributors.

3) First release scope (max 8 tasks)

1. Device gateway: an engine installed locally that connects to MQTT and Modbus and pushes readings.

2. Time-series store: storing energy/voltage/current readings with compression and time retention.

3. Live dashboard: real-time and historical consumption charts per site and device.

4. Rules and alerts engine: thresholds, schedules, and notifications (email/webhook).

5. Sites, devices, and users management: hierarchical organization and permission roles.

6. REST + WebSocket API: read/write points and integrate external systems.

7. Basic reports: estimated energy cost and daily/weekly reports exportable to CSV.

8. One-click deployment: a Docker/binary package to run the gateway and the free cloud version.

4) Pricing and profit model

• Free: up to 3 sites / 25 devices / 30-day data retention — a permanent free start with no card.

• Growth (monthly/yearly): more sites and devices, longer retention, advanced alerts, email support.

• Business: multi-tenant, SSO, unlimited retention, SLA, advanced carbon/cost reports.

• Enterprise/On-Premise: annual license for self-hosting with support and updates.

• Additional channels: a paid templates and components marketplace, and commission on installation services via integrator partners.

• Pricing principle: price by devices/sites, not by users, to encourage teams to expand.

5) Global rollout plan via free channels

• Open source on a public repository: the gateway and engine layer free to attract contributors and integrators.

• GitHub + community forum: device-connection examples, templates, and an integrations library.

• Educational content (SEO): guides and blogs such as "cut your electricity bill with load monitoring," translated into English and Arabic.

• YouTube and Reddit/Discord communities: installation tutorials and real-world examples.

• Local integrator partnerships: referral commission instead of paid ads.

• Developer program: expanded free access in exchange for publishing integrations and templates.

• Device marketplace: free templates that create viral adoption loops.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• IoT security: poorly secured devices — the solution is message signing, certificates, and virtual network isolation.

• Data privacy and information protection: compliance with laws such as GDPR and local data regulations — encryption, data minimization, and a Local-first mode that lowers risk.

• Automation liability: a rule error could disconnect a critical load — the solution is human approval for risky operations and an audit log.

• Licensing: an original product written from scratch with a clear license to avoid any legal ambiguity with similar tools.

• Technical scaling: data pressure and response time — choosing a suitable time-series database and designing for horizontal scalability.

• Cloud dependence: avoid it through Local-first design and features that work offline.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Voltara
