Rawi — Complete Project Plan

We turn your community's scattered sources into a reliable weekly newsletter with human editing and AI.

Complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Maintainers of open-source language and framework communities who produce a weekly newsletter manually and struggle to collect, verify, and draft links.

• Institutions and tech companies that want an internal or external newsletter for their developers without building an editorial system.

• Local teams and emerging communities that need a newsletter in a local language with full right-to-left support.

• Developer readers who want a reliable weekly summary instead of following dozens of sources.

Problem: Tech community newsletters are built manually today, with scattered sources, slow processes, and no scalability or localization. Our product turns this process into an automated editorial production line with human oversight.

2) Differentiators (Why choose our product)

• Multi-source ingestion engine: Releases, Issues, RFCs, package registries, RSS, forums, and social networks, with deduplication.

• Human editing first: AI suggests and drafts, and the editor approves and edits; no blind automated publishing.

• Native localization: multilingual interface and newsletters with RTL support from the ground up, not as an afterthought.

• Open outputs: static export, HTML, and email, without locking in data or templates.

• Source transparency: every item carries its link, license, and context, with copyright alerts.

• Works with any ecosystem, not just one language, expanding the market from day one.

• Free to start for open-source communities, with no credit card.

3) Scope of the First Version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Initial ingestion connectors: RSS, GitHub Releases/Issues, one package registry, and manual links.

2. Deduplication, topic classification, and importance ranking based on engagement and recency.

3. Editorial dashboard: approve/reject/edit items, with traceable assisted drafting.

4. Weekly issue builder with a ready-made template, RTL support, and preview before publishing.

5. Subscriber and consent management (Double opt-in) and unsubscribe.

6. Email delivery via an external provider + static web version export.

7. Automatic weekly scheduling with editor reminders and pre-generated drafts.

8. Rights protection: mandatory source link, license tagging, and warnings for disallowed content.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free forever: open-source projects and non-profit communities up to a certain subscriber limit, with product branding.

• Pro: monthly subscription for individual editors and small communities; more sources, scheduling, and localization.

• Team/Org: multiple seats, permissions, custom templates, analytics, and branding removal.

• Managed hosting as an optional add-on, and one-time setup services.

• Additional revenue from optional issue sponsorships with full transparency, not from selling subscriber data.

5) Global Go-to-Market Plan with Free Channels

• Publish the open-source repository on GitHub with Arabic and English documentation and ready-made examples.

• Launch on Hacker News, Reddit, dev.to, Mastodon, and Product Hunt.

• Collaborate with existing tech newsletter maintainers through issue swaps, not copying.

• Live demos in Discord, Telegram, language communities, and framework forums.

• SEO content: "How to build your community newsletter in an hour" guides and practical comparisons.

• Short YouTube videos showing the transformation from scattered links to a published issue.

• Ambassador program for early contributors and local starter kits for universities and Meetups.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

Legal:

• Content and quotation rights: use short summaries with attribution, check licenses, and have a fast takedown policy.

• Source terms of use and APIs: respect Rate limits and ToS and prefer official sources.

• Privacy and email: explicit consent, easy unsubscribe, and compliance with GDPR and local data protection laws.

• Generated content errors: mandatory human review and a clear disclaimer.

• Trademarks: review names and logos before launch.

Technical:

• Scraping fragility and API changes: a swappable connector layer and failure monitoring.

• Rate limits and blocking: caching, queues, and retries.

• Email deliverability: authenticated domains with SPF/DKIM/DMARC and complaint monitoring.

• Ranking bias and weak localization quality: periodic evaluation and human feed
