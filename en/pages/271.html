Maktabati — Complete Project Plan

Your digital library, organized and ready to read anywhere, and it starts free.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, growth plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

An original product plan for managing digital libraries and reading

1) Target Audience

• Individuals who own large e-book libraries (EPUB/PDF/MOBI) and want to organize them and access them from any device.

• Readers who want to track their progress and their collections across phone and computer without being tied to a paid account.

• Small teams: book clubs, school libraries, research groups, and those interested in personal archiving.

• Users looking for a simple, fast alternative that works locally or via self-hosting, without complex setup.

2) Differentiators (Why choose our product)

• A new architecture built from scratch: a modern database, and a unified API, with no legacy code.

• Instant in-browser reading with a full experience (progress, bookmarks, notes) without mandatory download.

• Lightweight sync of reading progress and notes across devices without a paid intermediary.

• Smart cataloging based on automatic metadata extraction (title, author, cover, series) and correction of common errors.

• Instant search by title, author, and the internal content of books.

• Works in two modes: local offline (Local-first) or one-click self-hosting.

• A native Arabic experience with full right-to-left support and Arabic text formatting in the reader.

• Free to start with a generous personal space that suffices for most users.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Uploading book files and importing them from a folder or a batch import profile.

2. Automatic extraction of metadata and cover with the option of manual editing.

3. A filterable and sortable library (author, category, series, reading status).

4. Instant text search in titles and metadata.

5. An in-browser reader with progress tracking, bookmarks, and dark mode.

6. Syncing reading progress and notes across devices.

7. A single user account with secure authentication, and optional public links for sharing.

8. Exporting the library and data to a portable format (backup).

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: limited storage, one user, and basic sync for two devices — with no time limit.

• Pro plan (monthly/yearly subscription): more space, multiple users, unlimited sync, connecting your own cloud storage, audit log.

• Teams/Enterprise plan: advanced permissions, branding customization, priority support.

• Additional sources: Managed Hosting for users who do not want to manage servers, for a monthly fee.

• A fixed principle: files belong to the user, and full export capability in all plans to prevent lock-in.

5) Global growth plan via free channels

• Open-source repositories and public code publication to build trust and attract contributors.

• Self-hosting communities (tech forums, Reddit, Hacker News) with short demos.

• Tech community rooms (Discord/Matrix) to provide live support and answer questions.

• Educational articles about organizing digital libraries and improving the reading experience (long-term SEO).

• Short videos on YouTube and video platforms: "From chaos to an organized library in minutes".

• Collaborations with book clubs and bloggers to try and review the product.

• Sharing anonymous usage statistics as a transparent case study to attract attention.

• A community plugin store that allows external contributions to boost organic growth.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

Legal:

• The risk of hosting or distributing copyright-protected content via public sharing links → a clear notice-and-takedown policy (DMCA), blocking public indexing by default, and placing responsibility on the user.

• Privacy regulations (GDPR/CCPA) require transparency in handling data and the right to deletion and export.

• The paid add-on must clearly separate personal storage from pirated content.

Technical:

• The disparity of file formats (EPUB/PDF) and the difficulty of consistent rendering → unification through a single rendering engine.

• Cataloging performance with huge libraries → incremental indexing and background jobs.

• Sync security and protection of user data → encryption in transit and at rest.

• Scalability as users grow while keeping hosting costs low.

• Backup failure or data loss → periodic backups and always-available export.
