Nasq — Complete Project Plan

Make your game easily, and share it with the world directly.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, distribution plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Beginners in game development: Those who want to make their first game without advanced programming complexity.

• Independent developers: Those who need fast production while maintaining performance.

• Educational institutions: To use it as a simple educational tool in game development courses.

• Small teams: A designer and a developer who want to collaborate quickly without complex setups.

2) Key Differentiators (Why Choose Our Product)

• Fast setup experience: Users can start working immediately without long installation steps or complex configurations.

• Integrated visual interface: Provides a simple level editor without relying on external tools.

• Web-first focus: Direct export to WebAssembly for easy sharing and global publishing.

• Free to start: All core tools are free, allowing everyone to try without financial barriers.

• Modular design: Allows adding components only as needed, keeping it lightweight and performant.

• Clear and simple documentation: Written in straightforward language to lower the learning curve from day one.

3) Scope of the First Version (Maximum 8 Tasks)

1. Build the core: Develop a simple module system using Rust with WebAssembly support.

2. Flexible component system: Design an independent data system to organize objects easily.

3. 2D rendering engine: Support simple, fast graphics with stable performance.

4. Visual level editor: Create a simplified editor in the browser to add and edit objects.

5. Hot Reload: Enable instant viewing of changes during development to speed up iteration.

6. Basic resource system: Manage images and sounds easily without complications.

7. Ready-made examples: Provide 3 small game templates to teach usage quickly.

8. Free beta release: Launch the first version publicly directly through the browser.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Completely free to start: The basic version is available for free to everyone, with no restrictions on personal projects.

• Optional revenue model: A simple monthly subscription offering extra storage, exclusive templates, and custom publishing.

• Templates and components marketplace: A commission system for selling templates or components created by the community.

• Enterprise licensing: Special packages for schools or companies that need dedicated technical support.

5) Global Distribution Plan via Free Channels

• Educational content marketing: Publish short tutorials on educational video platforms in Arabic and English.

• Open community: Create a public forum to exchange knowledge and answer questions for free.

• Developer channels: Share updates through programming platforms and technical repositories.

• Collaboration with institutions: Offer the free version to schools to increase global adoption.

• Organic promotion: Encourage users to share their projects on the web to boost distribution.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legal risks: Ensure all design and technology components used are original, and avoid any resemblance in names or visual elements.

• Technical risks: Difficulty supporting WebAssembly on some devices; addressed by providing an alternative desktop version.

• Performance risks: Challenges with performance on low-end devices; addressed through modular optimization.

• Community growth risks: Growth may be slow; addressed by focusing on ease of use and free educational content.

7) Proposed Name and Marketing Slogan

Proposed name: Nasq

Marketing slogan: Make your game easily, and share it with the world directly.
