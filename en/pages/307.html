Rafeeq — Complete Project Plan

Rafeeq: Bring your family together around caring for your parents… once, without chaos.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the go-to-market plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

New product plan: a center for coordinating elderly care within the family

1) Target audience

• Primary persona: the eldest adult (35–60 years old) who practically leads the care of one of their parents, often managing this in parallel with their work and family.

• Secondary users: siblings and other family members, the assigned/home caregiver, and the family volunteer.

• Supporting audience: doctors and nurses in clinics, health insurance advisors, and chronic disease and dementia support associations.

• First markets: countries where geographically dispersed families are common and where healthcare systems involve many scattered reports (America, Europe, the Gulf, Canada, Australia), with support for multiple languages.

2) Points of differentiation (why choose our product)

• Solving the coordination problem, not the physical care problem: we gather the scattered threads (medication, appointment, document, siblings, insurance claim) into one space, instead of the family spending hours on calls and exchanging messages.

• Designed as a "family group", not a "patient record": every task and appointment has a specific owner among the children, clarifying who did what and who is late — reducing friction among siblings and preventing duplicated or neglected work.

• Doctor-ready in one minute: a single button produces a "handoff note" in plain language: current medications, changes, questions for the next appointment — instead of papers scattered on the table.

• Free at its core: registration, family sharing, and core features at no cost, because whoever faces the coordination burden does not need an extra financial barrier.

• Respecting family privacy: granular permissions (who reads, who edits, who only sees documents), and a "view-only" mode for disagreeing siblings.

• Works in a busy environment: customizable reminders, one weekly summary instead of ten notifications, and a simple interface suitable for non-technical people.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Shared medication log: adding medications, dosages, and prescribing doctors, with reminder alerts and a log of who took the dose.

2. Medical appointment calendar: clinic and test appointments, with an owner for each appointment, the location link/note, and who accompanies the patient.

3. Family task board: distributing care tasks (buying medication, arranging transport, following up on the bill) among siblings, with due dates and completion status.

4. Document vault: uploading and classifying reports, insurance documents, wills, and prescriptions, with granular access permissions.

5. Claims and insurance log: tracking claims, rejection cases, expected amounts, and important dates.

6. Doctor handoff note: generating a simple summary that can be copied/printed before the appointment or an emergency.

7. Member and permission management: inviting members with roles (editor / viewer / care assistant) and a simple activity log.

8. Alerts and weekly summary: notifications that can be grouped via email or text messages, and a weekly status summary for the family.

Out of scope for the first version: advanced AI, integration with electronic medical records, a standalone app for care technicians, and instant payment to providers.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever (Freemium): one family space, up to 6 members, medications, appointments, tasks, and core documents.

• Paid family plan (~$9–12 per month or ~$99 per year): unlimited members, advanced permission levels, an enhanced handoff note, a full activity log, export and reports, and priority support.

• Annual subscription as a stated default: a clear discount in exchange for commitment, because the family decision cycle is long.

• B2B2C partnerships: subscriptions funded by patient associations, health care plans, or employer care programs — free for the end user, funded by the partner.

• Future additions (later, not at the start): a marketplace of certified care providers, a standalone app for care coordinators, and paid reports for extended families across countries.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Multilingual SEO: practical articles ("How to distribute care tasks among siblings", "A list of questions for a doctor's appointment") in the languages of the target
