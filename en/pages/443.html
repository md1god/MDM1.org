Mirsad — Full Project Plan

An eye on the sky all night, from anywhere.

Full execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the rollout plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Astronomy and night photography hobbyists using Raspberry Pi devices.

• Home weather station and environmental quality monitoring owners.

• Schools, universities, and science clubs.

• Park and "Dark Sky" reserve managers and community observatories.

• Anyone wanting to monitor a garden/home overnight with timelapse storage.

2) Differentiators (Why choose our product)

• Full setup in under 15 minutes via a web interface, no command line.

• Automatic image processing: exposure, stacking, color correction, then Timelapse generation and share-ready GIF.

• Smart on-device event detection (meteors, nebulae, clouds, light trails) via a lightweight local model.

• Multi-camera dashboards with a timeline covering the full night.

• Privacy first: images stay local by default; cloud upload is optional and encrypted.

• Works with Pi, USB, and CSI cameras, with a full Offline mode.

3) First Release Scope (max 8 tasks)

1. Minimal OS image + one-click install script.

2. Scheduled capture module for Raspberry Pi/USB camera images.

3. Local web server (FastAPI) for live view and settings.

4. Image processing pipeline: exposure, stacking, white balance, Timelapse generation via FFmpeg.

5. Local storage scheduling with automatic cleanup by size.

6. Simple event detection engine (frame differencing) + Telegram/email alerts.

7. Timelapse gallery of scenes + secure share link.

8. Arabic/English documentation and a ready-to-flash SD card image package.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free open-source for full local operation (Free-to-start).

• Sky Cloud plan — $4/month: cloud storage, advanced AI detection, private sharing domain.

• Enterprise plan (schools/fields/reserves) — $29/month: multi-camera support, team alerts, remote maintenance.

• Additional revenue: community sponsorship, accessories store (lenses, protection, covers), donations.

5) Global Rollout Plan with Free Channels

• Open-source repository on GitHub with an attractive homepage and Arabic/English SEO.

• Detailed articles on Hackster and Instructables "Build your camera in 15 minutes".

• Build and comparison videos on YouTube and Shorts.

• Posts on Reddit (astrophotography, Raspberry Pi), Stack Exchange forums.

• Discord/Telegram community server + user testimonials.

• Participation in Hackaday Prize challenges and open-good competitions.

• Presence at local astronomy clubs and educational exhibitions.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legal: filming neighbors' property or air traffic → documenting observation boundaries and privacy notices, GDPR compliance and image-handling policies, respecting no-fly and no-filming zones.

• Technical: camera heat, condensation, dust, and lens wear at night, disk consumption from images, light creep from traffic, interface security (authentication, local-only connection by default), network and power dependence (mains power / small UPS).

7) Proposed Name and Tagline

Proposed name: Mirsad

Tagline: An eye on the sky all night, from anywhere.
