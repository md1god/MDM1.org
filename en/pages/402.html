Silent Box — Full Project Plan

Read your inbox as one conversation, and let the rest wait.

Full execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, launch plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: a personal email assistant as a conversation

1) Target audience

• Primary: users whose daily message volume exceeds their ability to keep up (businessmen and businesswomen, freelancers, graduate students).

• Secondary: small teams (2–10 people) managing a support inbox or shared email who want quick summaries and ready-made reply drafts.

• Tertiary: users with more than one email account (personal + work) who are tired of switching between inboxes.

• Defining behavior: they open email more than 10 times a day, struggle with long messages and attachments, and treat privacy as a decisive factor before adopting any tool.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• The conversation is not just the core — it is the way of reading: instead of opening and skimming a message, the user starts a dialogue with a thread of messages: "Summarize," "What do I need to do?," "Write a friendly reply."

• Privacy built into the architecture, not into marketing copy: on-device analysis by default, with an optional, explicit, and clear cloud mode for each user.

• One inbox for multiple accounts with smart priority triage (urgent / follow-up / FYI) without complicated manual rules.

• Email turned into tasks: every message automatically becomes a task with a due date that can be synced to the user's calendar.

• Silent reading mode: one consolidated alert per day instead of constant buzzing for every message.

• Price: free to start, no credit card required, no time-limited trial.

3) Scope of the first version (8 tasks maximum)

1. Connect two or more accounts via OAuth (Gmail, Outlook), read-only at first.

2. Display the full thread in a single chronological conversation interface.

3. Automatic short summary for each thread on arrival (max 70 words).

4. Reply draft assistant with three styles (formal, concise, friendly).

5. Automatic extraction of tasks and appointments with an "Add to calendar" button.

6. Smart initial triage: urgent / requires a reply / can be ignored.

7. Engine running locally on-device with optional cloud for business users.

8. One responsive web app (no native apps in this version).

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free: two accounts, limited daily summaries, 30 drafts per month.

• Plus – $7/month (or $58/year): unlimited accounts, unlimited instant summaries, tasks and calendar integration, longer templates.

• Pro – $15/month: advanced local mode, priority processing, custom reply templates, log export.

• Teams – $8/user/month: shared support inbox and message assignment between members.

• Additional revenue sources: a dedicated enterprise plan and integration partnerships (no intrusive ads at all, to protect trust).

5) Global launch plan with free channels

• Product directories: launch on Product Hunt, BetaList, AlternativeTo, Uneed, and daily AI tool listings.

• Content: SEO blog in English, Arabic, and French on "how to handle 200 emails a day," plus short clips on YouTube/TikTok/LinkedIn showing before/after summarization.

• Communities: Reddit (r/productivity, r/email), Hacker News (Show HN), and Facebook and Telegram groups for freelancers.

• Viral growth: every summary can be sent as a shareable third-party link (with your consent), and a referral system: one free month for each side.

• Partnerships: free integrations with Notion/task managers in exchange for mutual exposure, and publishing ready-made reply templates as copyable content.

• Arabic community: WhatsApp and Telegram groups for professionals, and posts on X platforms in Arabic.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: full inbox access requires strict compliance with OAuth policies and minimal scopes, clear and revocable user consent, and immediate deletion upon revocation.

• Privacy: message data is not used for default training; de-identification of texts before sending to the cloud, and immediate deletion upon revocation.

• Compliance: GDPR and CCPA (data deletion/export), and domestic or European hosting for customers concerned with digital sovereignty.

• Technical: changing email interfaces (Gmail API quotas), summarization capacity risks, and a slimmed-down local option as a fallback for cloud outages.

• Competitive: email providers' eagerness to ship similar features; mitigated by deepening conversation, privacy, and tasks rather than summarizatio
