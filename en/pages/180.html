Anwa — Complete Project Plan

A cloud platform for high-accuracy atmospheric simulation, run from your browser in minutes.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

Product plan: A cloud weather and climate simulation platform

1) Target audience

• Researchers and academics in atmospheric sciences who need high-accuracy simulation experiments without managing heavy infrastructure.

• Small and medium enterprises: precision agriculture, renewable energy (wind and solar power), shipping and logistics, and insurance.

• Municipalities and emergency management agencies that need accurate local forecasts for floods, storms, and heat.

• Developers and technology companies that want to integrate ready-made weather forecasts via API into their applications.

• Universities in emerging economies that lack high-performance computing (HPC) clusters.

2) Differentiators (why customers choose our product)

• Runs entirely from the browser: no installation or complex environment setup, just upload your data and press the run button.

• Managed cloud architecture: automatic on-demand execution on GPUs/CPUs instead of buying permanent clusters.

• Hybrid model (physics + machine learning): speeds up simulation by 10–100× while preserving physical accuracy.

• High-accuracy automatic downscaling to neighborhood or field scale using AI models.

• Open API and unified data standards to facilitate integration and interoperability.

• Real-time collaboration: share simulation scenarios and results with the team inside the platform.

• Transparent, scalable pricing with a free tier to get started.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. A core simulation engine built from scratch with basic physical elements (pressure, temperature, wind, humidity).

2. Upload of initial/boundary conditions via common file formats such as NetCDF and GRIB2.

3. AI-based spatial downscaling from low resolution to high local resolution.

4. An interactive map display dashboard (layers: temperature, wind, precipitation, pressure).

5. On-demand cloud execution with a job queue and status monitoring.

6. A REST API to call ready-made forecasts or run a scenario.

7. Export results in standard formats and share them via a public link.

8. A free account system with a monthly usage cap and usage metering.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start (Free Tier): a monthly quota of simulation hours, limited resolution, and API access with a low call limit.

• Researcher plan (Pro): a monthly subscription that raises resolution and computing hours and adds advanced data export.

• Team plan (Team): multiple seats, shared workspaces, and admin permissions.

• Enterprise plan (Enterprise): private deployment (private cloud/on-prem), service-level agreement (SLA), and dedicated support.

• Pay-as-you-go for peak periods and massive computing scenarios.

• Additional sources: integration marketplaces, and commercial licensing for partners.

5) Global go-to-market plan using free channels

• An open-source repository for the core engine to attract developers and researchers and build technical reputation.

• Free documentation and tutorials in Arabic and English with ready-to-run examples (notebooks).

• Scientific forums and communities (weather and climate groups, especially in Africa, Asia, and Latin America).

• Hackathons and university competitions to build applications on top of the API.

• Content on video platforms and technical articles explaining real-world use cases.

• Integration with open data platforms and supported research programs, and partnerships in global climate initiatives.

• A university ambassador program that grants expanded free access in exchange for promoting the platform.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: respecting the licenses of source data (ERA5 and the like), clarifying the limits of liability because forecasts are not a guarantee, and complying with data protection laws (GDPR) when processing user data.

• Legal: ensuring that all code and design are original, and avoiding any infringement of trademarks or patents.

• Technical: the accuracy of hybrid models may be unreliable for rare phenomena; the need for continuous validation.

• Technical: cloud computing costs may escalate; quotas and financial monitoring must be controlled.

• Technical: ensuring scalability and low response time under high demand.

• Technical: API security, key management, and abuse prevention.
