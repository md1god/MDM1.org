TerraWeave — Complete Project Plan

Build a credible climate scenario in minutes, without the need for supercomputers.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, dissemination plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not yet launched.

1) Target Audience

• Researchers and academics at universities with limited computational resources, especially in developing countries.

• Earth and climate science teachers in schools and universities who need practical experiments without HPC setup.

• Policy analysts and civil society organizations who want to compare emissions scenarios quickly.

• Startups and climate risk consultants who need traceable preliminary estimates before specialized studies.

• Data and model developers interested in reproducible science and open API interfaces.

2) Differentiators (Why Choose Our Product)

• No HPC: run reduced-complexity models on CPU or on-demand cloud computing.

• From scenario to result in minutes: a visual editor instead of complex configuration files.

• Reproducibility: every result is automatically linked to the model version, data, and random seed.

• Multilingual from day one: Arabic, English, French, and Spanish.

• Open data and a generous free tier: a genuinely free start, not just a short trial.

• Publication-ready outputs: PDF and CSV reports with metadata and automatic citation.

• Scalable: from a teaching project to enterprise API integration without changing the platform.

3) Scope of the First Version (Maximum 8 Tasks)

1. User account and project workspace with simple roles (owner/viewer).

2. Visual scenario editor: emissions, land use, policies, and time horizon.

3. Simplified simulation engine (atmosphere-ocean-land-carbon) running on CPU with a Python interface.

4. Library of 5 reference models with consistency tests and comparison against historical data.

5. Interactive results dashboard and CSV/PNG/PDF export with full metadata.

6. Project copying and sharing via permanent links and immutable versions.

7. Free usage limits and actual consumption metering (simulations/minutes).

8. Interactive tutorial in Arabic and English and short onboarding paths.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free forever: 3 low-resolution simulations per month, public projects, basic export.

• Researcher – $19/month: more simulations, private projects, unlimited comparisons.

• Lab/Classroom – $99/month: 20 seats, classroom spaces, group reports.

• Institution – $499/month: administrative permissions, SSO, higher limits, priority support.

• Enterprise/API: usage-based pricing + annual contract.

• Additional revenue: documented scenario packs, training courses, setup consulting, and research sponsorship from funders.

5) Global Dissemination Plan via Free Channels

• GitHub: open SDK, Jupyter examples, and API documentation.

• Hugging Face / Zenodo: reduced model weights and documented datasets.

• Free educational content: a YouTube video series and written lessons in multiple languages.

• University ambassadors: a free program for professors in exchange for workshops and case studies.

• Multilingual SEO: "how to calculate a climate scenario" articles and methodological comparison pages.

• Specialized communities: Discourse, Reddit, Mastodon, and Earth science forums.

• Hackathons and student competitions with in-kind prizes and free licenses.

• Partnerships with educational organizations and data libraries to publish free links.

• Short MOOC on an open platform that attracts users to the free plan.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

Legal:

• Data and reference model licenses: audit every source and ensure clear license compliance.

• Explicit disclaimer: results are estimates and not the sole basis for policy or financial decisions.

• Privacy and data protection (GDPR): data minimization, encryption, and deletion on request.

• Intellectual property rights for user-uploaded content and DMCA policy.

• Possible export restrictions on some sensitive data or models.

Technical:

• Accuracy of reduced models: document validity limits and continuously compare against references.

• Compute costs: strict limits, queues, and results caching.

• Reproducibility: version pinning and result signing.

• Data quality and provenance chain: automated validation of sources and time ranges.

• Security: isolation of simulation jobs, API rate limiting, and periodic review.

• Dependence on a single cloud provider: portable design and local operation.
