Nabr — Complete Project Plan

Speak with confidence and learn from every sentence you say.

A complete execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, growth plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target audience

• Adult learners (18–40) who want to speak with confidence, not just memorize grammar.

• Professionals, expatriates, and students preparing for interviews, travel, or study.

• Language test takers who need short, repeated voice practice.

• Arabic speakers learning English, learners of Arabic as a second language, then other languages later.

• Teachers and small schools as a secondary market through simple dashboards.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Short voice conversation (3–5 minutes) resembling daily life, not a long automated chat.

• Instant pronunciation correction at word and sound level, with a simple explanation rather than a vague score.

• Grammar correction after the turn ends, with natural, applicable rephrasing.

• Automatic flashcard generation from the learner's actual mistakes, linked to spaced repetition.

• Practical scenario packs: travel, work, interviews, tests, daily situations.

• Greater privacy: clear control over voice storage, instant deletion, and use without a permanent account.

• A lightweight experience that works from the browser like a mobile phone, with no installation or teacher appointments.

• A generous free start, and lower cost than group private lessons.

3) First version scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Optional login, and selection of goal, level, and target dialect.

2. Low-latency voice conversation engine with natural interruption and retry.

3. Three initial scenario packs: travel, work, and interviews.

4. Pronunciation feedback: distinguishing difficult sounds, with re-recording and comparison.

5. Grammar and pronunciation correction after the conversation with short alternative phrasings.

6. Automatic flashcard generation and spaced review scheduling.

7. A simple progress dashboard: pronunciation accuracy, recurring mistakes, and weekly commitment.

8. Full privacy settings, data export/deletion, and a responsive web experience (PWA).

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever: 10 minutes of conversation daily, one scenario pack, and basic feedback.

• Plus: $7.99 per month or $59 per year; longer conversations, all packs, and deeper analysis.

• Pro: $14.99 per month; test training, personalized plans, and exportable reports.

• Regional pricing automatically reduced according to purchasing power.

• Additional revenue sources: school subscriptions, separate test packs, and an enterprise API later.

• No ads in the first version, and no discount in exchange for selling voice data.

5) Global growth plan with free channels

• SEO pages for each scenario and language: “How to order coffee,” “Job interview in English,” with free audio examples.

• Short clips on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Reels: a daily pronunciation challenge and a before/after result.

• Reddit, Discord, and language-learning forums: free weekly challenges and useful discussions without spam.

• Launch on Product Hunt, Hacker News, and Indie Hackers with a clear free version.

• Partnerships with university clubs, language exchange groups, and independent teachers.

• Referral program: a free month of Plus for anyone who invites a friend who completes their first conversation.

• Localize the interface into Arabic early, then Spanish, French, and Portuguese to support global growth.

• Optimize ASO/PWA and visibility in “voice language tutor” and “pronunciation practice” results.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legally: Voice may be considered biometric or personal data; explicit consent, a defined retention period, instant deletion, and GDPR and CCPA compliance are required.

• Legally: Child-specific restrictions (COPPA); it is preferable to limit the first version to ages 16 and above.

• Legally: Scenario content and corrections must be original, with a review of usage rights and no imitation of trademarks.

• Legally: Transparency about the limits of AI, and a disclaimer regarding academic or professional decisions.

• Technically: Speech recognition systems are biased toward dialects; extensive testing and possible manual correction are required.

• Technically: Pronunciation assessment may be inaccurate; it must be presented as guidance, not a final judgment.
