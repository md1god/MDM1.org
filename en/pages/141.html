Nahla — Complete Project Plan

A lightweight in-browser simulation platform that trains drone swarms to fly autonomously before reality.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: a lightweight autonomous flight simulation platform that runs in the browser

1) Target audience

• AI and Reinforcement Learning researchers who need a fast, reproducible training environment.

• University students and professors in robotics and control, especially those who lack strong graphics hardware.

• Emerging drone companies that want to test navigation algorithms before real flights.

• Swarm developers and researchers in multi-agent collective coordination.

2) Points of distinction (why choose our product)

• Runs entirely in the browser via WebGPU/WASM without installing a heavy game engine or compiling large amounts of code.

• Focuses on swarms and multiple agents from day one, not as a later add-on.

• A Python interface compatible with Gymnasium/PettingZoo so it can run immediately in any existing training pipeline.

• Infinitely procedurally generated environments instead of static maps, to reduce overfitting.

• A "Sim-to-Real" pipeline built into the platform with unified model export.

• Free to start with no time limit, with full transparency in the core code.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. A realistic flight dynamics engine for a quadrotor with a fixed time step.

2. A browser-based 3D rendering engine (WebGPU) with one generatable urban scene.

3. Basic sensors: RGB camera, IMU unit, and a preliminary Lidar.

4. Running a small swarm of up to 10 vehicles simultaneously (Multi-Agent).

5. A Python SDK package compatible with Gymnasium and PettingZoo (step/reset interface).

6. Ready-made scenarios (obstacle navigation, target tracking, collision avoidance).

7. Session recording and export of training data in standard formats (rosbag-like + CSV).

8. A simple cloud dashboard for computations, projects, and sharing via link.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free-to-Start: local execution in the browser, a swarm of up to 10 vehicles, unlimited environments and scenarios.

• Pro tier (monthly subscription): cloud computing for long training, a larger swarm, project storage, support priority.

• Enterprise tier: On-Premise deployment, integration with CI systems, support contract and service-level guarantee.

• Add-on marketplace: commission on environments and sensors developed by the community (Open Core).

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Publish the core as open source on GitHub with documentation and examples ready to run in a minute.

• Publish the package on PyPI and interactive demos on Hugging Face Spaces.

• Tutorial series on YouTube and written lessons that show training a drone in under 5 minutes.

• Presence in ROS, Reddit, and Discord communities and running Challenges with symbolic prizes.

• Writing papers and academic contributions and holding university competitions to build an initial user base.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: the dual-use nature (security/civilian) may be subject to export restrictions; the need for clear terms of use and legal review for restricted countries. Compliance with civil aviation regulations is also required when describing any Sim-to-Real path.

• Technical: the Sim-to-Real Gap may weaken the credibility of results.

• Technical: WebGPU performance limits on weak devices may restrict swarm size and fine-grained physics.

• Technical: the difficulty of matching the accuracy of long-established physics engines without high development costs.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: Nahla (نحلة)

Marketing tagline: A lightweight in-browser simulation platform that trains drone swarms to fly autonomously before reality.
