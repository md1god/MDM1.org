Nabda — Complete Project Plan

Your fitness and nutrition at home, with your data and complete freedom.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the go-to-market plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: an open platform for tracking fitness, nutrition, and weight (self-hosted first)

1) Target audience

• Privacy-conscious individuals who refuse to link their health data to closed commercial servers.

• Self-hosting and free software communities (homelab, r/selfhosted).

• Fitness coaches and nutritionists who need a customizable system for their clients without exorbitant fees.

• Teams and small clinics that want data on their own infrastructure with legal compliance.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• The user owns the data: full export in open formats (JSON/CSV) and seamless import, with no data lock-in.

• Works without internet: a Progressive Web App (PWA) that works offline and syncs later.

• Lightweight architecture: a single Docker container + PostgreSQL, running on a home device or a small VPS.

• API-first: a documented REST interface and sandbox, with support for integration with smartwatches via open protocols.

• No-code customization: a drag-and-drop workout table editor, plus RTL support and full translation from day one.

• Truly free to start: the free core with no feature limits, monetizing services rather than blocking the basics.

3) Scope of the first version (max 8 tasks)

1. Logging daily exercises and building flexible training schedules (sets/reps/weights).

2. An expandable exercise library with muscle and equipment tags.

3. Tracking weight and body measurements with time-series charts.

4. Nutrition tracking: calories + macros + an editable database of common foods.

5. Web app + mobile PWA with offline operation and sync.

6. Documented REST API + Webhooks + a plugin system.

7. Multiple users, roles and permissions, and linking a coach to their clients.

8. Full data export/import + encrypted backup and a one-time setup command.

Suggested tech stack: Go or Python for the backend, PostgreSQL, Next.js/React frontend, Docker containers, OpenAPI documentation.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free version (self-hosted): all core features free forever, with a free license that prevents commercial enclosure.

• Managed cloud (Freemium): a free tier with a simple storage limit, then monthly plans (e.g. $5 / $10 / $20) for automatic sync, backup, and support.

• Teams/clinics plan: per-user subscription + single sign-on (SSO) + reports.

• Support and consulting + a Marketplace for paid plugins (developers keep a percentage).

• The principle: free to start, and you pay for convenience and maintenance, not for core features.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Publishing on GitHub with a strong README, illustrative images, and a live demo.

• Listing in awesome-selfhosted, Awesome Privacy, and AlternativeTo.

• Coordinated launches on Hacker News, Product Hunt, and Reddit (r/selfhosted, r/QuantifiedSelf, r/opensource).

• Technical content: articles on "health data ownership" on Dev.to, Hashnode, and small YouTube channels.

• Open communities: Lemmy, Mastodon, and Discord, with a contributor program and symbolic rewards.

• Community localization and translation via Weblate to open new markets with no marketing budget.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

Legal:

• Health data privacy: compliance with GDPR and similar, encryption in transit and at rest, and a clear privacy policy.

• An explicit medical disclaimer (the product is not a diagnostic or treatment tool).

• Free license compliance and preventing commercial re-enclosure, and patent screening on names and logos.

• Not making unproven medical marketing claims.

Technical:

• Sync conflicts between multiple offline devices, solved via CRDT or timestamps.

• Self-hosting security (weak user configurations) → secure-by-default templates and automatic updates.

• Scalability with high data density, and data migration testing.

• Quality, diversity, sources, and licensing of the food database.

7) Suggested name and tagline

Suggested name: Nabda (نَبضة)

Tagline: Your fitness and nutrition at home, with your data and complete freedom.
