Sāha — Complete Project Plan

Build your multiplayer game without managing servers: instant rooms, synchronized state, and one-command deployment.

Implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

1) Target audience

• Indie game developers and small teams that want multiplayer without building servers.

• Web developers experienced only in front-end interfaces, with no networking or backend experience.

• Game Jam organizers, programming students, and hackathon participants seeking quick setup.

• Casual and turn-based game producers and private-room games.

• Teams building interactive non-game applications: live quizzes, collaborative boards, discussion rooms.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Focus on casual and turn-based games: deterministic, more precise state synchronization instead of a heavy general-purpose solution.

• Built-in reconnection and session recovery by default, multiplayer's most fragile part.

• Global edge deployment with one command, without managing servers or databases.

• Open protocol and open-source SDK to prevent vendor lock-in.

• Generous permanent free hosting tier, with smooth upgrades as you grow.

• Right-to-left interface support and localization within the dashboard and templates.

• AI assistant to generate room logic and rules from a text description, with ready-made tests.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Client and server SDK in TypeScript with a server-authoritative state model.

2. Room lifecycle: create, join, leave, and automatic closure.

3. Quick player matchmaking and private rooms via invite code.

4. Player presence, reconnection, and state recovery after disconnection.

5. State snapshots and restoration when needed.

6. Dashboard: logs, room metrics, and runtime errors.

7. CLI tool for local and global deployment with one command.

8. Three ready-made templates and interactive documentation: card game, quiz, collaborative room.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Permanent free: 1,000 active rooms per month, 50 concurrent players, 1GB storage, community only.

• Pro — $19 per month: 20,000 rooms, 500 concurrent, longer logs, custom domain.

• Growth — $99 per month: 100,000 rooms, 2,000 concurrent, service-level agreement, email support.

• Enterprise — custom pricing: regional data residency, security audit, priority support.

• Additional revenue: managed hosting, migration support, and a template marketplace that shares revenue with the community.

• No startup fees and no credit card required before upgrading.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Open-source the core on GitHub with a clear license and instant run templates.

• SEO-oriented documentation solving specific problems: reconnection, state synchronization, player matchmaking.

• Discord community and free support channels, with a bot explaining getting started and collecting feedback.

• Presence at Game Jams (such as itch.io and Ludum Dare) with a free template and symbolic prizes.

• Educational content on YouTube, DEV.to, Reddit, and HTML5 communities, in English and Arabic.

• Launch on Product Hunt and Hacker News with a no-signup demo.

• Partnerships with universities, coding clubs, and free workshops to build a first multiplayer game.

• Ambassador program: free upgrade for those who contribute an approved template or lesson.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Privacy: comply with GDPR and CCPA, delete player data, and define retention periods.

• Children's data: apply COPPA rules when targeting younger ages and prevent unnecessary data collection.

• Content and compliance: content moderation policy, and restrictions on virtual gambling and loot boxes by country.

• Intellectual property and licensing: review contribution licenses, and clarify ownership of user content.

• Geographic residency: regional data residency options for enterprise customers.

• Latency and scale: global latency testing, and clear limits for distant regions.

• Synchronization and cheating: server authority, input validation, and suspicious pattern detection.

• Attacks and cost: rate limiting, DDoS protection, and spending caps that prevent surprise bills.

• Compatibility: WebSocket restrictions on some networks and mini-apps, with a Long-polling fallback.

• Version fragmentation: release policy and backward compatibility to avoid breaking published games.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Sāha

Tagline: Build your multiplayer game without managing servers: instant rooms, synchronized state, and one-command deployment.
