Nabd Al-Wajhat — Complete Project Plan

A single weekly newsletter that summarizes for you the most important in the world of frontend interfaces, without noise and without cost.

A complete execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the distribution plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

An original product plan: a weekly email newsletter for frontend developers

1) Target Audience

• Frontend developers (beginners and intermediate) who don't have time to follow scattered sources.

• Frontend engineers in small teams and startups who want a concise, high-density update.

• JavaScript/TypeScript, React, Vue, and Svelte developers and those wanting to follow performance and accessibility (a11y) news.

• Students and career switchers looking for organized Arabic/English content.

2) Points of Distinction (Why choose our product)

• Editorial quality, not automated volume: every issue passes through human editing that filters out noise and orders content by value, not by trend.

• A reading experience from scratch: an original, lightweight, fast design, dark mode, and a clear reading-time estimate for each section.

• Multi-format content from a single source: email + static web page + RSS feed + short audio summary, all generated from one system written from scratch.

• Full transparency: an open public archive, and a "no sponsors" mode activatable from subscriber settings.

• A community, not just a broadcast channel: a weekly discussion via Discord and a curated readers' reply in each issue.

• A completely free start with no paywall, with an optional paid track for professionals.

3) Scope of the First Version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. A simple email subscription system (form + Double Opt-in confirmation + unsubscribe).

2. A source aggregator (RSS/APIs) with automatic categorization and tags, and a blocklist for low-quality sources.

3. An editorial dashboard for selecting and ordering items and writing the editorial comment for each item.

4. An original email template compatible with major email clients and responsive on mobile.

5. A public static archive page indexable by search engines + RSS feed.

6. Single-language support at first (English) with a structure ready to expand later.

7. Basic tracking (opens/clicks) that respects privacy without third-party trackers.

8. A publishing guide and a weekly scheduled sending system with a backup of issues.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free: all issues, the archive, RSS, and community discussion.

• Pro plan (optional): the "frontend professionalism" track — in-depth interviews, source code for examples, and project files, at a low monthly price or a discounted annual one.

• Ethical sponsorship: one sponsor slot per issue, no tracking, with a clear label and transparency about the advertiser.

• A paid jobs board for companies, free for developers.

• Principle: no paywalled content inside the main email; payment is always optional.

5) Global Distribution Plan Using Free Channels

• Open content via SEO: every issue is published as a static web page linked to a title/tag, to attract organic search.

• Smart republishing: quotes and selected points on dev.to, Hashnode, and Medium with a link to the original.

• Community platforms: useful (non-promotional) posts on Reddit, Hacker News, Discord, and Stack Overflow when relevant.

• Short video: 30–60 second clips of the best idea in the issue on YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram.

• GitHub: an open archive repository + free templates to attract developers, and a launch via Product Hunt.

• A connector network: inviting authors and open-source projects to exchange co-written issues.

• A growth loop built into the product: a share button for each section + a simple referral link without annoying incentives.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

Legal:

• Content rights: not copying others' texts; limiting to short quotation + the link + original commentary, and relying on licensed or open sources.

• Privacy: complying with GDPR/CCPA when collecting email, allowing full deletion, and minimizing data collection.

• Sponsorship: clearly disclosing business relationships to avoid misleading.

• Trademarks: avoiding any name or logo similar to existing projects.

Technical:

• Email deliverability: setting up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and maintaining a low complaint rate.

• Dependence on external source APIs: building an abstraction layer and caching to avoid outages.

• Scalability and cost: email services with tiered pricing and cheap static infrastructure.
