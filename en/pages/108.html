Rihal — Complete Project Plan

Your maps in your pocket, they work without internet and respect your privacy.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the outreach plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not been launched yet.

Product plan: an open maps application that works offline

1) Target audience

• Travelers, wayfarers, and hikers who lack network coverage in remote areas.

• Cyclists and long-haul drivers looking for routes without consuming their data plan.

• Relief and disaster-response teams that need reliable maps when connectivity is cut.

• Privacy-conscious users who oppose tracking and data collection.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Light and fast: lower launch time and lower battery and memory consumption than comparable apps.

• Smart download of regions on demand with shaded maps (terrain layers) offline.

• Optional anonymous social sync to share routes with friends.

• An authentic right-to-left Arabic interface with support for accurate local fonts.

• Edit data locally, then upload it when connectivity returns (offline editing).

• No ads or tracking, with full transparency about what is sent (nothing by default).

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Display the map locally and download one geographic area for testing.

2. Instant search by name and coordinates within downloaded regions.

3. Calculate walking/cycling/driving routes offline.

4. Determine location via GPS and display it even in airplane mode.

5. Record the trip route and save important waypoints.

6. Manage downloads: delete/update regions and clear space.

7. Support the Arabic language and full RTL direction.

8. Privacy foundation: no tracking, no mandatory account, and data export/import.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: unlimited worldwide map downloads and no ads.

• "Optional support" with a symbolic subscription granting: instant region updates, cloud sync, and route backups.

• A free tier for small organizations, and a paid plan for teams (relief/tourism) that includes internal map deployment.

• No data selling, no targeted ads. Revenue comes only from support and subscriptions.

5) Global outreach plan using free channels

• Publishing on free app stores with a description optimized for search engines (ASO).

• Contributor communities on OpenStreetMap, Github, Reddit, and traveler forums.

• Partnerships with hiking and cycling clubs and environmental associations (mutual support, no charge).

• Free educational content: route guides and short video blogs.

• A local ambassador program whose members recommend the app in their countries in exchange for a recognition badge.

• "The app does not connect to the internet" campaigns to demonstrate privacy in practice.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: the map data license requires clear attribution and not imposing additional restrictions; it must be complied with.

• Legal: data protection laws (GDPR and the like) require transparency even when no collection takes place.

• Legal: some countries impose survey licenses or restrictions on distributing border maps.

• Technical: conflicting reference coordinates between systems require precise handling.

• Technical: the size of map files and the storage/network cost on low-end devices.

• Technical: offline route guidance accuracy is limited compared to cloud services.

• Technical: maintaining continuous updates for rapidly changing data (new roads/closures).

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Rihal

Tagline: Your maps in your pocket, they work without internet and respect your privacy.
