Bariq — Full Project Plan

Turn RAW files into eye-catching images in seconds.

Full execution plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, launch plan, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

1) Target Audience

• Amateur photographers who bought a DSLR or mirrorless camera and cannot process RAW files.

• Professional photographers (weddings, products, reportage) who need fast batch processing.

• Content creators on YouTube and Instagram relying on eye-catching photos.

• University and media college students, and local photography clubs.

• Small agency staff and merchants who display many products.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• 100% local processing: edits run on the user's device with no uploads, protecting privacy and speeding up work.

• One-click smart assistant: Smart Looks analyze lighting and tones, suggesting custom corrections per image.

• Two-level interface: "Quick" mode for beginners, "Precise" mode revealing curves, layers, and details for pros.

• Instant before/after with a fully undoable (non-destructive) change counter.

• Wide camera code support via a community-updatable database.

• Batch processing and multi-export with reusable template settings.

• Lightweight and fast: built on modern technologies (WASM + WebGPU on the web, Tauri on desktop) for low memory use.

• A truly free starting version, no watermark.

3) Scope of the First Release (8 tasks maximum)

1. Basic RAW reading engine supporting the top 20 camera brands (via DNG and raw formats).

2. Core correction tools: exposure, white balance, contrast, lighting, saturation, and detail sharpness.

3. A non-destructive editing system with change history and undo/redo.

4. Instant before/after view with split screen and 100% zoom.

5. 12 ready-made smart editions (Smart Looks) applied with one click.

6. JPG/PNG export with quality/size options, plus batch export.

7. One web app running in the browser (supports sRGB and Adobe RGB).

8. A free account with local project saving (localStorage) and free camera database updates.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free (Free-to-start): all core correction tools + 20 full-quality exports per month + the camera database, no watermark.

• Pro – Subscription: $6/month or $48/year — unlimited export, batches, custom settings, high bit-depth TIFF, optional encrypted sync.

• Pro – Lifetime: $129 one-time (sold during early launch campaigns at $79).

• Agency reseller: $199 per year for 5 devices with shared management.

• Extra revenue: paid look templates (Marketplace, 30% commission for designers) and a short paid training course.

5) Global Launch Plan Using Free Channels

1. YouTube: "how to turn RAW into a professional image in 5 minutes" in many languages (English, Arabic, Spanish, French).

2. TikTok/Instagram Reels: short visual before/after with photography hashtags.

3. SEO blog: direct comparison articles like "best free RAW processing software 2026" and fixes for common problems.

4. Reddit and photography forums: useful tips without direct advertising.

5. Facebook and Telegram groups for non-English-speaking photography communities.

6. Launch on Product Hunt, Hacker News, and AlternativeTo with limited launch offers.

7. Influencer program: free Pro versions for photography content creators in exchange for honest reviews.

8. Discord/Forum community for feature requests, feeding the best into a public roadmap.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

Legal:

• Patent risk in image processing and defect correction (noise removal, spectral); mitigation: openly licensed algorithms plus legal review before launch.

• Licensing of camera decoding libraries (some restrict commercial use); mitigation: well-licensed open source and code written from scratch.

• Trademarks: audit the name and logo globally before registration (WIPO/IPA).

• Privacy compliance: GDPR/CCPA, a clear notice that processing is local, no uploads without consent.

• Export restrictions on encryption/artificial intelligence software in some countries.

Technical:

• Huge variety of camera codes and color profiles → inaccurate colors.

• In-browser performance (WASM/WebGPU) may drop on weak devices.

• Fierce competition from established free tools → difficulty acquiring and retaining users.

• Cost of training/running artificial intelligence models for the smart editions.

• Loss of user trust if a camera database update fails.

7) Proposed Name and Marketing Tagline

Proposed name: Bariq (بَريق)

Marketing tagline: Turn RAW files into eye-catching images in seconds.
