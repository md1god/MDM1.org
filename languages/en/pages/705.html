Original product plan: a coverage path planning platform for autonomous agricultural vehicles

The problem: autonomous agricultural vehicles (tractors, harvesters, and spraying robots) need paths that cover the entire field without overlapping chemical application and without missing areas, with safe turns and obstacle avoidance. Most available solutions are complex to integrate.

1) Target audience

1. Emerging agricultural robotics manufacturers (OEMs): companies building harvesting, spraying, and weeding robots that want a ready-made planning engine instead of building one in-house, saving 6–12 months of development.

2. Agricultural service providers and cooperatives: those managing a fleet of 3 to 20 machines who need automatic field division among vehicles to finish before the weather changes.

3. Drone spraying companies: need paths for irregular fields that account for tank capacity and battery.

4. Researchers and agricultural robotics engineering students: need a free, quick-to-learn tool for field experiments and research publication.

5. The tech-savvy farmer (early adopter): owners of 50–500 hectare fields using commercial guidance systems who want a better plan that reduces fuel and overdosing.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product?)

1. Unprecedented ease of use: the core is a Python package with a REST API and an interactive web app; integration in an hour instead of weeks, and try it directly from the browser.

2. Fleet division: automatic division of the field among multiple vehicles with time balance (±5%) and clear delivery points — a feature missing from what's available today.

3. 3D terrain awareness: using DEM elevation data to make paths follow contour lines on slopes, reducing water runoff.

4. Cost optimization, not just geometry: the goal is reducing euro/hectare (fuel + time + chemical overlap), with an analytics dashboard for coverage, overlap, distance, and expected time.

5. Open standard compatibility: export GeoJSON, GPX, KML, ISO 11783 (ISOBUS), and ROS 2 messages, integrating with guidance and farm management systems.

6. Free to start: an open-source core under a permissive license (Apache-2.0) and a free cloud tier — the student and the manufacturer start from the same door.

3) First-release scope (max 8 tasks)

1. Task 1 — Engineering engine: read field boundaries from GeoJSON, KML, and Shapefile, convert coordinates WGS84 ↔ UTM with meter precision, and validate the polygon.

2. Tasks 2 and 3 — Paths and headland: Boustrophedon pattern with automatic best-angle detection, spiral pattern, and contours; with a Headland margin using three turn patterns (Omega, half turn, Fish-tail) and selecting the best based on equipment.

3. Tasks 4 and 5 — Obstacles and ordering: automatically divide the field around fixed obstacles (trees, poles, ponds) and connect regions with transition paths; then order paths to minimize non-working distance (approximate TSP) and export to GeoJSON, GPX, CSV, and ROS 2.

4. Task 6 — Web app MVP: draw field boundaries on a MapLibre map, enter equipment width and speed, generate and preview the plan, and export it, with free calculations.

5. Task 7 — Analytics: coverage percentage (%), overlap (%), total distance (km), expected time (hours), and display layers over the map.

6. Task 8 — Documentation: a "first plan in 10 minutes" guide, OpenAPI docs, and 6 examples including an L-shaped field and one with two internal obstacles.

4) Pricing and revenue model

1. Open-source core (Apache-2.0): free forever; builds trust and community and a free marketing channel.

2. Free cloud tier: 3 saved fields and 20 plans per month with all first-release analytics — enough for students and small farms.

3. Pro tier — $19/month: unlimited fields, fleet division, 3D terrain, ISOBUS export, and email support within 48 hours.

4. Business tier — $99/month: REST API with 10,000 calls per month, 5 users, and priority support.

5. Enterprise contracts: on-premise installation in the client's servers with a 99.5% SLA — from $3,000 to $10,000 per year.

6. Professional services: OEM engine integration and team training ($500–$800 per day) — direct revenue that funds early development.
