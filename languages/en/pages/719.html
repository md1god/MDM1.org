EU AI Act compliance within reach of every small business, with audit-ready evidence.

Full implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, business model, rollout, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

Product plan: EU AI Act compliance assistant for small and medium-sized businesses

1) Target audience

• Small and medium-sized enterprises in the EU (10–249 employees) that use or develop AI systems: marketing agencies, recruitment firms, clinics, e-commerce stores, and software companies. They represent about 99% of EU establishments (roughly 23 million companies).

• Companies outside Europe that sell AI services or outputs inside the EU, such as Gulf, Turkish, and UK companies, because the law applies extraterritorially when outputs are used in the European market.

• The non-specialist responsible: owner, CFO, or operations manager who handles compliance part-time and cannot afford 5,000–50,000 euros in law-firm fees.

• Independent consultants and small accounting firms serving 10–50 clients needing multi-client workspaces and reusable reports.

• Data protection officers (DPOs) in mid-sized companies who need a documented record and audit-ready evidence in minutes.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• "Evidence first": every answer or classification automatically generates a documented record (date, responsible party, source, version) instead of scattered files. Example: classifying a recruitment system as "high risk" creates evidence linking it to Annex III.

• Classification based on explicit rules and literal references to articles, not on a language model's guess; the language model is used only for explanation and translation, reducing legal "hallucination".

• Privacy by design: local in-browser processing where possible, EU hosting, encryption, and no use of customer data for training — a critical requirement for clinics and lawyers.

• Multilingual support from day one (Arabic, English, German, French, Spanish), while competing tools are mostly English.

• Free to start with no credit card, and export in open formats (PDF/CSV/JSON) with no data lock-in.

• Tracking of the changing timeline: alerts for 2025–2027 deadlines and updates when new guidance or standards are issued.

3) First-release scope (max 8 tasks)

1. AI systems registry: fields (purpose, vendor, data, users, country, decision type) with CSV import and quick export.

2. Risk classification wizard: branching questions that determine prohibited/high-risk/limited/minimal, with a reference to each article and annex.

3. AI literacy records (Article 4): short 10–15 minute training units, certificates, and an exportable attendance log.

4. Transparency obligations check (Article 50): questions about generated content, chatbots, and deepfakes, with ready-made disclosure templates.

5. Evidence vault and audit package export: file upload, timestamp, versioning, and change log, then PDF/CSV/JSON including a gap summary and closure plan.

6. Task board and reminders and supplier questionnaire: owner and deadline per task, and public links to collect and document supplier declarations.

4) Pricing and business model

• Permanent free plan: one user, up to 3 systems, basic PDF export. Goal: build trust and spread before paid conversion.

• Starter: 19 euros/month per organization, up to 10 systems, unlimited evidence, and 3 users.

• Growth: 49 euros/month, unlimited systems, supplier questionnaires, full audit log, and priority support.

• Consultant: 99 euros/month for multi-client workspaces (up to 15 clients) and export with the consultant's branding.

• Annual discount equals two months free, and 50% for non-profits and universities.

• Additional revenue: paid templates, certified training, and a 20% referral commission for accounting firms. Example: 1,000 organizations at an average of 29 euros = 348,000 euros annually.

5) Global rollout plan via free channels

• Multilingual SEO: guides targeting phrases such as "Article 4 AI Act" and "Annex III list", with free tools as lead magnets to collect emails.

• Free tools with no sign-up: classification calculator, downloadable deadline calendar, and disclosure templates; each tool ends with a call to create an account.

• Partnerships with chambers of commerce, small-business associations, and incubators in Germany, France, Spain, and Gulf countries.

• Community content: LinkedIn, Product Hunt, compliance and data-protection forums, and joint webinars with specialized lawyers.
