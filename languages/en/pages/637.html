StrideLens — Full Project Plan

Find your photos from every race in seconds, and give the photographer a fair return.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, go-to-market plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target audience

• Independent sports photographers and small studios: they capture thousands of photos per race and need to sell them quickly.

• Athletes: runners, cyclists, triathletes, obstacle course racers, and participants in walking races and marathons.

• Race organizers and sports clubs: a distribution and partnership channel to attract photographers and participants.

• Priority for local race markets with high participation, then global expansion by language and event.

2) Points of differentiation (why choose our product)

• Dual search: face + bib number together for more accurate results, with no app required.

• Privacy first: no storage of selfie photos, automatic deletion of embeddings, explicit consent, and an option to opt out of indexing.

• Low commission for the photographer with pricing freedom, and the photographer retains ownership of their photos.

• Free to start: search and preview are free for the athlete, and a free monthly balance for the photographer.

• Fast experience: indexing within minutes, watermarked previews, and instant purchase and download.

• Multilingual and multicurrency support, and shareable event pages with QR.

• Full transparency in earnings and fees, and a simple dashboard.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Photographer registration and batch photo upload with event data and EXIF.

2. Automatic indexing pipeline: face detection + OCR for bib numbers with manual correction.

3. Athlete search by selfie or bib number, and display of results with watermarked previews.

4. Cart and secure payment and instant download of high-resolution copies.

5. Photographer dashboard: pricing, sales, commission, and payouts.

6. Privacy and compliance tools: consent, deletion, opt-out of indexing, and data deletion.

7. Public event pages + QR + social sharing links.

8. Basic administration: content review, reporting, and sales analytics.

4) Pricing and profit model

• Athletes: search and preview are free; purchase a single photo or an event package, with a one-time payment.

• Photographers:

• Free: free monthly photo balance + 12% commission.

• Starter: low monthly subscription + larger balance + 10% commission.

• Pro: higher subscription + nearly unlimited balance + 8% commission + custom watermark.

• Race organizers: free event page, and an optional package for promotion and support.

• Revenue sources: commission on sales + subscriptions + optional services.

• Free-to-start: no credit card required, and no setup fees.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Local SEO: pages for each race titled something like “photos [race name] 2026” with structured data.

• Free partnerships with running clubs and local race organizers.

• Reddit, Facebook, Strava, and Discord groups specialized in running and photography.

• Photographer communities and forums, and Lightroom groups.

• Short content on TikTok and Instagram Reels: “how to find your photo after the race.”

• Referral program with free rewards for photographers and athletes.

• Launch on Product Hunt and specialized directories.

• Cold outreach to race organizers with free samples and a demo event page.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal:

• Biometric data is subject to laws such as GDPR, BIPA, CCPA/CPRA, and PDPL; explicit consent and a clear retention and deletion policy are required.

• Image rights and licensing: ownership belongs to the photographer, and a clear usage license for the athlete.

• Minors’ privacy: indexing disabled by default or requiring guardian consent.

• Taxes, VAT, and payments through a trusted provider such as Stripe or Paddle.

• Content liability and reporting of abusive images.

• Technical:

• Variation in face recognition accuracy depending on lighting, angle, and skin tone; a minimum confidence threshold and human review.

• False positives and similarity between faces; clear preview before purchase.

• OCR errors when bib numbers are distorted or covered; manual correction.

• Storage and processing costs; batch processing and smart compression.

• Data security and breaches; encryption in transit and at rest.

• Demand spikes after races; scalable architecture and processing queues.
