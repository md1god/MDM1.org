HelioWorks — Full Project Plan

From solar data to discovery in minutes.

Full implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the rollout plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: solar data analysis through an open cloud platform

1) Target audience

• Solar physics researchers and university professors who analyze satellite and observatory data.

• Graduate students and amateur observers of planets and the Sun.

• Space Weather forecasting specialists at space weather agencies and solar energy companies.

• Teachers and educational content creators who need ready-made examples to present.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• A cloud interface that works with click and drag, while the SDK stays in Python for those who want to program.

• Unified access to data from several observatories with a Cache that prevents massive re-downloads.

• Automatic preprocessing: time alignment, units, and coordinates with no manual setup.

• An AI assistant that turns a scientific question into analysis code you can edit and verify.

• Notebooks and shared dashboards in one click, with reproducible results.

• A ready-made plotting library for common solar shapes (heat maps, spectra, time series) at publication quality.

3) Scope of the first version (8 tasks maximum)

1. Free user account with GitHub/Google login.

2. Retrieving data from two major solar observatories with chunked and cached downloads.

3. A unified loader layer that returns ready objects with the correct units and coordinates.

4. Four core preprocessing operations: time cropping, grid resampling, smoothing, and normalization.

5. A browser-based plotting editor with five ready templates and PNG/PDF export.

6. Converting natural-language questions into runnable, editable Python code.

7. Saving projects and sharing them via a public link with a version history of results.

8. Arabic/English documentation with three practical lessons and an installable package on GitHub.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: open source for the core Python package, 5 GB of storage, and 100 analysis operations per month.

• Pro: a monthly subscription (around 15$) for more space, processing on faster servers, and an unlimited AI assistant.

• Research teams: multiple seats, shared management, and institutional invoicing (around 40$/user/month).

• Enterprises: On-premise deployment with an annual license — the core future revenue.

• Side income: paid training and academic sponsorships.

5) Global rollout plan with free channels

• A public repository on GitHub with a clear README and pip install within a minute.

• Writing an open documentation paper (JOSS-style) and publishing it in physics and astronomy communities.

• A series of short tutorials on YouTube and YouTube Shorts in multiple languages.

• Posts on X, LinkedIn, and Reddit (r/solarphysics, r/Python) with striking visual results.

• A community push on Discord to solve issues and gather feedback.

• Ready example notebooks on Google Colab and Kaggle to attract students.

• Taking part in free conferences and workshops (NIYAZ, Astronomy Education Days) as a speaker.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: checking the data licenses of each observatory with mandatory source Attribution; avoiding agency logos in the brand identity; protecting the trademark by registering the name.

• Technical: the huge data volume may raise storage costs — handled by caching and partial processing; changes in external data interfaces — handled by a pinned version layer; scientific validity of results requires a reference comparison against trusted sources.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: HelioWorks

Tagline: From solar data to discovery in minutes.
