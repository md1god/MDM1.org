Lumira — Complete Project Plan

Turn your film scans into fully colored images in your browser, for free and with no installation.

Plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, business model, distribution, risks. Under development; not launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Analog enthusiasts: 35 mm and 120 shooters who scan at home with a camera or scanner and want correct color negatives without density curves or color science.

• Professional photographers and small studios: 50–200 client frames weekly, needing consistent batches that save hours of manual work.

• Archives and families: converting thousands of faded or damaged negatives into preserved digital copies, needing gentle restoration that keeps film grain.

• Beginners and students: have lab scans, don't know how to make positives, want a good one-click result and a clear Arabic interface.

• Modest devices: no powerful PCs or graphics cards; need a browser tool on an old PC or phone, with no install.

2) Differentiators (Why Choose Our Product)

• Free, no install: works in the browser on drag-and-drop, with no setup, install guides, or security warnings.

• Scientific auto color: removes the orange negative mask per channel sensitivity, builds the image in density space, and simulates film and paper without manual color picking.

• Roll as one unit: upload a whole roll at once; every frame gets consistent exposure, contrast, and color, with one-click single-frame edits.

• Privacy first: processing stays on-device via WebGPU and WebAssembly by default; no upload unless the user opts into sync.

• Fast non-destructive editing: local project with undo/redo, dust/scratch tools that keep grain, export to JPEG, TIFF, WebP, Contact Sheets.

3) Scope of the First Version (max 8 tasks)

1. Arabic drag-and-drop UI for JPEG, TIFF, PNG, DNG, processed locally in-browser.

2. Negative pipeline: per-channel orange mask removal and adjustable density curve.

3. Recipe library for 6 common films (color, B&W, slides), customizable.

4. Roll-level batches with automatic exposure, contrast, and color consistency.

5. Auto dust/scratch removal and manual brush preserving grain.

6. Darkroom basics: exposure, contrast, color balance, local dodge/burn.

7. Export with ICC files, template filenames, and Contact Sheets.

8. Non-destructive local projects with undo/redo and a three-step tutorial.

4) Pricing and Business Model

• Free tier: 50 frames/month, all basics, no watermark; enough for two full rolls.

• Pro: $6/month or $48/year for unlimited frames, batches up to 500, advanced paper recipes, optional cloud sync.

• Studio: $99 once, multiple seats, branded export, priority support.

• Educational exemption: free for schools and students with verified educational email, building loyalty and reviews.

• Ancillary revenue: referral commission on film/scanner sales from partner stores, optional in-app donations, no intrusive ads.

5) Global Distribution Plan with Free Channels

• SEO content: articles and before/after demos on "how to scan a negative" plus per-film pages for steady free traffic.

• Communities: give real help in Reddit, Discord, and Facebook analog groups; show results, not ads.

• Short video: YouTube lessons turning a ruined frame into a beautiful image in under a minute, with known amateur photographers.

• Open source: release the processing core as a free library on GitHub to attract developers and integrations.

• Local partnerships: film labs, photo clubs, and schools; distribute a simple printed guide in Arabic and English.

• Multilingual localization: Arabic and English first, then Japanese, German, French, Spanish, Korean via volunteers, with localized search pages.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Trademarks: film names owned by big firms cannot be our brand; use generic descriptions, user-named recipes, no logos.

• Licensing and privacy: free core license; cloud sync with explicit permission and minimal storage under European data rules.

• Intellectual property: no copied ICC files or protected curves; build from published tables and document derivation.

• Browser compatibility: uneven WebGPU support; fall back to WebAssembly and CPU with longer runtime.

• Color quality: accurate color on old or unusual films is hard; mitigate with manual controls and user feedback.

• Performance and security: large files may exceed browser memory; no uploaded code executes; scan dependencies continuously.

7) Proposed Name and Marketing Line

Proposed name: Lumira

Marketing line: Turn your film scans into fully colored images in your browser, for free and with no installation.
