Molvista — Full Project Plan

From molecule to discovery — smart analytics in minutes.

Full implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the go-to-market plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

New product plan: deep learning analytics for drug discovery

1) Target audience

• Researchers in pharmaceuticals and computational biology at universities and research institutes (especially from emerging countries that lack expensive infrastructure).

• R&D teams in small and medium drug-development startups that need advanced tools without investing in servers and deep learning specialists.

• Graduate students and professionals in quantum chemistry and materials who want ready-made, reusable models.

• Biology consultants and companies that order analytics services with cloud support.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• No-code drag-and-drop interface: upload SMILES/FASTA/PDB files and get ready predictions within minutes, with a Python API option for professionals.

• Custom pre-trained models for common tasks (binding peak, toxicity properties, protein thermal stability prediction) — no need to train from scratch.

• Automatic comparative benchmark: runs several models on the user's data, automatically selects the best one, and provides a reliability card and explanatory explanation.

• Collaborative environment: sharing experiments and result logs among team members with built-in version tracking (MLflow-style).

• Lightweight Arabic and international data support, direct integration with public data sources, and full privacy preservation for sensitive data.

3) First version scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Data upload and format unification service (SMILES/SDF/FASTA) with automatic cleaning.

2. Three ready-made tasks: binding peak prediction, toxicity classification, materials property prediction.

3. Library of pre-trained models callable with a single line.

4. Interactive results dashboard: charts, accuracy metrics, and feature importance explanations.

5. Documented REST + Python SDK.

6. User and team management with free quotas.

7. Local mode (Docker) to run fully without the cloud to protect data.

8. Help pages + a guided tutorial for a new user within 10 minutes.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free to start: 3 tasks per day, two pre-trained models, fully open local client — attracts researchers and students.

• Pro — $29/month: unlimited tasks, advanced models, experiment log, processing priority.

• Firm — $199/month: teams up to 10 members, API with higher rates, executive reports, priority support.

• Consulting services and enterprise training + partnerships with research institutes as an additional income source.

• Keep the core model open source to build trust, and earn revenue from the cloud and advanced features.

5) Global go-to-market plan with free channels

• Educational content: a series of short clips and practical lessons in multiple languages (English, Arabic, Spanish, Chinese) on YouTube and GitHub.

• Academic presence: workshops at university conferences, project codes for graduate students, and open article pages.

• Community: active Discord/Forum + answering questions on Stack Overflow and Reddit (r/chemistry, r/bioinformatics).

• Open template catalog: ready-to-execute examples runnable with one click (recipes) that users can copy and publish.

• Referral program for researchers: free credit in exchange for inviting a colleague, and partnerships with open source communities.

• Search engine optimization (SEO) for task pages and common scientific terms.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: predictions are not medical advice or regulatory approval — a clear disclaimer must be added; compliance with the licensing of data used and its sources; strict privacy policy for sensitive data.

• Legal: avoid trademark and license infringement in the deep learning libraries used.

• Technical: overclaimed prediction quality can harm trust — publish independent evaluations and clear model limitations.

• Technical: model obsolescence as data changes; difficulty integrating non-standard biological files; increasing compute costs with growth.

• Business: the need for continuous adoption by the researcher community; existing specialized competition.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: Molvista

Marketing tagline: From molecule to discovery — smart analytics in minutes.
