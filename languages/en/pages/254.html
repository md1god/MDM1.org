Mizan Pay — Complete Project Plan

All payment networks and account statements in a single pipeline.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, target audience, product, revenue model, distribution plan, and risks. The project is under development and has not yet been launched.

Product Plan: A unified platform for initiating payments and reconciling account statements

1) Target Audience

• Fintech engineers and small Payment Service Providers (PSPs) who want to send ACH/SEPA/FedNow/SWIFT/RTP payments without building validators for each network.

• Small neobanks and Treasury teams that need to automatically match camt account statements with payment requests.

• Developers of accounting systems and ERPs who want to generate bank payment files and read account statements within their products.

• Cross-border e-commerce companies and freelancers that work with multiple currencies and payment networks.

2) Key Differentiators (Why Choose This Product)

• Full sandbox environment: Mock banking data and an account statement generator, allowing developers to get started on day one without a bank contract.

• Automated reconciliation: Automatically links payment requests to camt statement movements and provides a ready-to-use discrepancy report, rather than simply generating files.

• Field-level accuracy: Validates IBAN/BIC/routing numbers, due dates, and amount limits, with clear error messages that reference the specific field number.

• Can be self-hosted or deployed as a managed cloud service: No vendor lock-in or forced reliance on a single provider.

• Strictly typed SDKs (TypeScript/Python) and a CLI, with interactive documentation and a comparison of reference ISO 20022 formats.

• Built-in audit trail and compliance data preparation (AML checks) as a preliminary step before sending.

3) Scope of the First Release (Maximum of 8 Tasks)

1. REST API for generating and validating pain.001, pain.008, and camt.052/053/054 messages.

2. Rules engine to validate account numbers, currencies, dates, and amount limits before generation.

3. Adapters for four networks: ACH, SEPA, FedNow/RTP, and SWIFT, with an output file for each network.

4. Reconciliation interface to match account statements with payment requests and display discrepancies.

5. Sandbox environment with a mock banking data generator and ready-to-test account statements.

6. CLI, npm package, and Python package with ready-to-use examples.

7. Lightweight web dashboard: payment history, reconciliation status, and validation errors.

8. Interactive documentation, basic role-based access control, and API keys.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Core is open-source (MIT license): Free for local use and testing forever — a free-to-start model.

• Free: Up to 1,000 messages per month on the cloud + unlimited sandbox access.

• Pro — $49/month: 25,000 messages, automated reconciliation, report exports, and email support.

• Scale — $299/month: Near-unlimited messages, advanced audit trail, SLA, and ERP integration.

• Self-hosted / Enterprise: Annual license + support and updates for ISO 20022 formats.

• Additional revenue: Integration consulting (onboarding) and paid training courses.

5) Global Go-to-Market Plan via Free Channels

• Open-source GitHub repository + Show HN and Reddit (r/fintech, r/programming) to reach developers.

• SEO-focused comparison articles: "pain.001 vs pain.008", "How to read camt.053" — to drive long-term organic traffic.

• Dev.to, Medium, and YouTube: Short tutorials such as "Create your first SEPA payment in 10 minutes".

• X/LinkedIn: Technical threads in English and Arabic about banking integration challenges.

• Product Hunt at cloud launch + free guides in community repositories.

• Local Telegram/WhatsApp groups for priority markets (Gulf, Egypt, Germany), plus participation in meetups and hackathons.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legal: The product does not hold funds — must avoid classification as a money transmitter. AML/KYC compliance remains the responsibility of the client. Also must consider GDPR and account data protection, sanctions screening, and data privacy laws by jurisdiction (e.g., Saudi Arabia and UAE data protection laws).

• Technical: Variations in ISO 20022 schema versions and bank-specific requirements; mapping complexity; timing issues, cut-off times, and business day rules; API key security and message spoofing; and future schema updates requiring backward-compatible releases.

• Operational: Reliance on a single cloud provider and difficulty supporting multiple local networks without partners.
