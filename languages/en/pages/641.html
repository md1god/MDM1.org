ShelfSignal — Complete Project Plan

A book discovery platform built on evidence from real technical discussions, not paid recommendations.

Implementation plan for a new project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, growth plan, and risks. The project is under construction, not launched.

1) Target Audience

• Developers and engineers seeking reliable books on programming, systems, and technical leadership.

• Founders and product owners wanting sources based on real discussions.

• Self-learners needing structured reading paths, not random lists.

• Content creators and technical communities seeking objective recommendation signals.

• Learning and development teams in small and medium firms.

2) Differentiation (Why choose our product)

• Evidence before recommendation: each book links to contextual excerpts and the original discussion, not just mention counts.

• Smarter signal: ranking combines mention frequency, source quality, recency, and diversity, with manipulation detection.

• Full transparency: every recommendation shows its source and date; commercial content is labeled.

• Paths, not lists: graded reading collections by topic and level, saveable and shareable.

• Free start: basic browsing and exploration need no payment or registration.

3) First-Version Scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Build a scheduled data pipeline from official APIs for public technical communities, respecting rate limits.

2. Extract book titles and match them with Open Library and Google Books for ISBN and metadata.

3. Unify editions, remove duplicates, and build search-engine-indexable book pages.

4. Develop an initial ranking algorithm based on discussion count, quality, and recency, filtering repeated mentions.

5. An exploration interface with search, topical tags, time filters, and manually curated reading paths.

6. Show short contextual excerpts with the source link and why the book was mentioned.

7. A free account to save books, follow topics, get a weekly summary, and report errors.

8. An admin dashboard for merge oversight, error correction, quality monitoring, and basic analytics.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free forever: unlimited browsing, search, trends, saving up to 20 books, and a weekly summary.

• ShelfSignal Pro: $5 per month or $39 per year; unlimited alerts, advanced filters, CSV export, and personal analytics.

• Team/Education: $12 per user per month; shared collections and a reading management dashboard.

• Revenue: Pro subscriptions, affiliate links to bookstores with clear disclosure, sponsorship of labeled reading paths, and a paid API later.

• Principle: no paywall on core community data, and no paid ranking within results.

5) Global Growth Plan with Free Channels

• Programmatic SEO: long-tail pages such as "best books on operating systems according to developer discussions," with structured data.

• Community launch: publish on Reddit, Lobsters, dev.to, and technical Discord communities, with content explaining the methodology, not just a link.

• GitHub as trust: open-source the data pipeline and evaluation tools to build credibility and attract contributors.

• Weekly newsletter: "Signal of the Week" with one book and evidence of its appearance in discussions.

• Product Hunt, X, and LinkedIn: coordinated launch with stories about books that changed developer decisions.

• Free partnerships: technical blogs, small podcasts, and learning communities through shared reading paths.

• Short video: "Why are developers talking about this book?" clips reusing excerpts with attribution.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legal: comply with API terms, avoid storing full copyrighted content, and use only short excerpts with attribution.

• Data rights: review community data rules and clarify attribution, reporting, and removal policies.

• Affiliate and advertising: disclose commercial links and sponsorships, and visually separate them from organic ranking.

• Privacy: minimize data collection and comply with GDPR for saving, alerts, and account deletion.

• Technical: ambiguous titles and duplicate editions; solve with ISBN matching and human review.

• Source limits: API changes or restrictions; solve with multiple sources, caching, and failure monitoring.

• Manipulation: artificial signals or spam; solve with anomaly detection, per-source limits, and periodic review.

• Quality: incorrect extraction or misleading context; solve with confidence scoring, "uncertain" labels, and a correction dashboard.

7) Proposed Name and Tagline

Proposed name: ShelfSignal

Tagline: A book discovery platform built on evidence from real technical discussions, not paid recommendations.
