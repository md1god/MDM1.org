Horizon — Complete Project Plan

The sky in your hands, simpler than you imagine.

Complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the distribution plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not yet been launched.

Original Product Plan

1) Target Audience

• School and university students: those who wish to understand the sky as part of their studies.

• Amateur observers: those who use the naked eye or telescopes to watch celestial objects at night.

• Teachers: those who want a simple tool to explain celestial phenomena inside or outside the classroom.

• Travelers: those who wish to get to know the sky in new places far from light pollution.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Mobile-first: designed specifically for outdoor use, not limited to desktop devices.

• Focuses on what is actually visible: highlights only objects visible to the naked eye or through telescopes, instead of showing every possible object.

• Smart educational mode: explains why each object appears at its time, with a quick answer to the question "What is this?"

• Very lightweight: runs efficiently on low-end devices and favors fast loading.

• Simple at first glance: requires no prior experience to start using it immediately.

3) First Version Scope (8 tasks maximum)

1. [ ] Location and time: automatic detection of current place and time.

2. [ ] Simplified sky dome: display of visible objects only according to time and place.

3. [ ] Planet identification: highlighting planets visible to the naked eye with a clear name.

4. [ ] Direction guide: adding a direction indicator to facilitate comparison with the real sky.

5. [ ] Quick search: ability to search for a celestial object to find the best time to view it.

6. [ ] Comfortable night mode: calm colors that preserve night vision.

7. [ ] Visibility time display: showing when an object becomes visible or disappears.

8. [ ] No-account mode: using all core features without forcing the user to create an account.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Start: completely free, while keeping all core observing features.

• Revenue source: optional educational package for teacher groups, sold separately without breaking the free experience.

• No ads: no reliance on intrusive ads to preserve use in the dark and inside classrooms.

• Transparency: any paid feature will be optional and completely clear.

5) Global Distribution Plan Using Free Channels

• Educational communities: participating in teacher groups and free science initiatives to spread the tool.

• Stargazing groups: collaborating with local groups to introduce it to new amateur observers.

• Short educational content: creating clips that show how to identify a star or planet in one minute and publishing them for free.

• Description optimization: using clear, natural descriptions to increase discovery via search engines.

• Free tools lists: adding the tool to the free educational tools lists circulating among teachers worldwide.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

· Type · Risk · Mitigation ·

· Legal · Use of non-open data · Relying exclusively on explicitly open data, or on astronomical calculations to compute sky positions. ·

· Legal · Trademark or design similarity · Fully inventing the name, logo, colors, and icons from scratch, avoiding any borrowed elements. ·

· Technical · Accuracy of calculations over time · Periodic testing by comparison with the real sky at different times, focusing on accuracy for visible objects only. ·

· Technical · Slowness on low-end devices · Relying on 2D rendering first, reducing graphics, and optimizing performance before adding any effects. ·

7) Proposed Name and Tagline

Proposed name: Horizon

Tagline: The sky in your hands, simpler than you imagine.
