Nabta — Complete Project Plan

Nabta turns free satellite imagery, weather, and soil data into precise irrigation and fertilization decisions per field, without technical expertise.

Full implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, growth, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

Product plan: A farm decision platform using space-based sensing

1) Target audience

• Small and medium farmers (under 50 ha) in arid and semi-arid regions, especially MENA, sub-Saharan Africa, and South Asia.

• Cooperatives and farm associations managing members’ fields and needing a unified dashboard.

• Agricultural engineers and field advisors advising hundreds of farmers.

• Donors and local governments monitoring drought, productivity, and sustainability.

• Buyers, financiers, and insurers seeking verifiable water, carbon, and risk reports.

2) Why choose our product

• Ready product, not a research toolkit: web, mobile, and API, with no machine learning expertise or complex setup.

• Effectively free start: Sentinel-2, Landsat, and open weather data, so no commercial imagery subscription at launch.

• Built for the field: Arabic, French, and English interfaces, offline mode, WhatsApp/SMS alerts, low data use.

• Actionable recommendations, not just maps: weekly irrigation, fertilization doses, and crop stress alerts with causes.

• Transparent confidence: each recommendation includes a confidence score, sources, and last-updated date; advisors can challenge and correct the model.

• Farmer data ownership: data is not sold; farmers can export or delete it; models run locally where possible.

• Ready sustainability reports: exportable water and carbon outputs for buyers and financiers, not unverified figures.

3) First-version scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Field definition: draw a GPS polygon or import boundaries, with automatic suggestions from Sentinel-2 imagery.

2. Crop health: NDVI/NDMI time series and anomaly detection (drought, stress, growth decline) with alerts.

3. Irrigation schedule: weekly recommendation using FAO-56, weather forecasts, and estimated soil moisture.

4. Fertilization: NPK doses based on soil analysis, growth stage, and crop type.

5. Yield estimation: mid- and end-season forecasts with a confidence range.

6. Pest/disease alerts: local risks based on weather and regional extension reports.

7. Water and carbon report: shareable PDF/CSV with water footprint and sustainability indicators.

8. Field app: offline mobile app with later sync and WhatsApp/SMS alerts.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free: up to 5 ha, one user, basic NDVI monitoring, monthly alerts.

• Pro: $3/ha per season (minimum $15), including irrigation, fertilization, yield estimation, and reports.

• Cooperative: $199/month for up to 500 ha, with multi-farm dashboard and advisor roles.

• API/enterprise: usage-based pricing ($0.02 per call) and annual contracts for insurers, buyers, and institutions.

• Additional revenue: certified sustainability reports, low-cost sensor integration, training and onboarding.

5) Global growth plan with free channels

• Extension/cooperative partnerships: free one-season offers for feedback and field recommendations.

• Free education: short Arabic, French, and English courses on YouTube, TikTok, and blogs, with real cases.

• Developer/open-data communities: release an SDK and open-source client library; join remote sensing and Open Data hackathons.

• WhatsApp/Telegram: free bot for quick inquiries and farmer outreach, leading users to the app when needed.

• Universities/students: free platform use for graduation projects and competitions, creating local ambassadors.

• NGOs: free offers for food security and climate adaptation programs, with donor-ready impact reports.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: commercial review of satellite and weather terms required; farmer privacy and land rights may cause disputes; advice needs a clear disclaimer; carbon verification standards may change.

• Technical: cloud cover and image gaps limit accuracy; 10-meter resolution is weak for very small fields; model drift across regions/crops; poor connectivity in remote areas; low-cost sensor calibration; growing storage and processing costs.

• Mitigation: combine sources, publish confidence scores, periodic field validation, clear data contracts, and an emergency plan if free sources fail.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Nabta

Marketing tagline: Nabta turns free satellite imagery and weather and soil data into precise irrigation and fertilization decisions for every field, without technical expertise.
