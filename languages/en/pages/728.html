Siraj — Complete Project Plan

Siraj: a question that lights up the mind, and a minute of viewing earned with merit.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue model, rollout plan, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

1) Target Audience

• Families with children aged 4–12 who own an Android TV and want to organize viewing time without daily conflict.

• Parents who do not want to create accounts or connect devices to cloud services, and who want children's data to stay in the home.

• Homes with weak or intermittent internet, since the product works entirely locally without a permanent connection.

• Children who love immediate rewards: every correct answer gives viewing minutes they feel they earned through effort.

• Schools and small learning centers that may later need a multi-device version, but not initially.

2) Differentiators (Why Choose Us)

• Privacy by design: no cloud, no account, no data collection; all questions, files, and statistics are stored locally and encrypted.

• Two-minute setup: a QR code appears on the screen; the parent scans it from a phone browser on the same network, with no registration or password.

• Minute economy: a correct answer grants one to three minutes; a wrong answer opens a short explanation, then an alternative question.

• Adaptive learning: three difficulty levels and five core subjects, with recent questions automatically repeated to reinforce concepts.

• Native Arabic experience: full RTL interface, clear fonts, and large buttons suitable for remote control and young children.

• Free to start with no ads: the basic version is fully functional; payment is only for additional content.

3) First Release Scope (Max 8 Tasks)

1. Build the Android TV app with Compose for TV and local storage via Room, with full Arabic support.

2. Create multiple child profiles locally, each with a name, avatar, and optional PIN code.

3. A question engine with 5 subjects × 3 levels, including 300 ready-made questions as a starter pack.

4. Minute wallet: rewards are credited immediately on answering, deducted during viewing, and stop at zero.

5. A built-in video player supporting device files and USB, with a visible timer and clear stop button.

6. A parental dashboard over LAN using QR and an encrypted session, to edit questions and daily limits.

7. Protection: database encryption, data-export prevention, and manual local backup.

8. Content packs downloadable and importable from a file, without a central store or mandatory connection.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free version: all core functions, one child profile, 300 questions, no ads or mandatory subscription.

• Additional subject packs: $2.99 per pack (100 questions), including math, science, and Arabic.

• Lifetime family license: $19.99, unlocking all packs, 6 child profiles, and updates for two years.

• School license: $99 per year for 30 devices, with local reporting tools and centralized installation.

• No data selling and no ads; profit comes only from content and licenses, preserving trust and compliance.

5) Global Rollout Plan with Free Channels

• Publish a free version on Google Play, F-Droid, and a direct website as an APK, with a landing page in six languages.

• Target Android TV, XDA, and Reddit communities with honest walkthroughs showing the two-minute setup.

• Short videos on YouTube and TikTok: "How does your child earn viewing time with a question?" with a real demo.

• Partner with parenting blogs, teachers, and community schools for free licenses and unpaid reviews.

• SEO for phrases such as "screen time control for children" and "educational TV app," targeting 10,000 installs in 6 months.

6) Risks (Legal and Technical)

• Legally: COPPA and GDPR-K compliance is achieved by not collecting personal data, but this must be clearly documented in the privacy policy.

• Content rights: questions must be written in-house or use licensed content, avoiding protected curricula or images.

• Store policies: children's apps require accurate age rating and periodic review to avoid suspension.

• Technically: Android TV version fragmentation may break the control interface; test on at least 10 reference devices.

• Local network security: the QR and parent session could be exploited; require encryption, expiration timeout, and same-network access restriction.

• Timer bypass or data loss: address with hidden settings, password lock, and automatic weekly local backup.
