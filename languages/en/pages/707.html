Lexora — Complete Project Plan

Learn a word once in context, and it will stay in your memory for a long time.

Complete execution plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue, growth, and risks. Under construction, not yet launched.

Original English vocabulary learning product plan

1) Target audience

• Self-directed learners targeting IELTS 6.5–8.0 in 3–9 months, knowing 2,000–4,000 words but forgetting them when writing or speaking.

• University students and professionals aged 18–35 in Egypt, the Gulf, South Asia, and Eastern Europe, where English is required to graduate or advance.

• Teachers and schools needing ready-made class vocabulary sets with exportable progress reports.

• Immigrants and new residents needing everyday and interview vocabulary, not only academic lists.

• Learners on mid-range or older Android phones preferring 5–10 minute sessions and a lightweight offline app.

2) Points of distinction

• Context first: words appear in 3-sentence scenes with images in 40–60 seconds; recall is measured in context, not isolated translation.

• Our adaptive scheduling: review intervals use response time, confidence, and error type, not just success or failure, cutting unnecessary reviews about 30%.

• Semantic word map: groups of 10–12 related words (e.g., environment, economy) and confused pairs (affect/effect) to reduce interference.

• Production before consumption: learners write or say a new-word sentence and get brief correction, turning passive knowledge into use.

• Free tier: 400 words and unlimited review, without locking core learning behind a subscription.

• Progressive web app (PWA) working offline, under 25MB, with adjustable-speed British and American pronunciation and no audio purchases.

3) First-version scope (max 8 tasks)

1. 1,200 core words tagged CEFR B1–C1, academic and everyday.

2. Three short scenes per word (3,600), commercially licensed images, in-house definitions.

3. British and American TTS with IPA and 0.75×–1.25× speed.

4. Daily 10-minute session + adaptive scheduling + streaks and retention stats.

5. Production exercise: write a sentence, then rule-based correction, with optional LLM assistance.

6. Instant search + word map + similar-pair cards.

7. Web and Android PWA with cloud sync and CSV/Anki export.

8. Progress dashboard: retention rate, weak words, and weekly study time.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free forever: 400 words, unlimited review, 3 new words daily, no ads.

• Pro: $4.99/month or $29/year: all words, 300 monthly writing corrections, offline packs.

• Founding offer: $69 lifetime for first 1,000 users, funding the first 6 months.

• Teachers: $2.5 per seat/month, minimum 20 seats, with class dashboard and reports.

• Mix: 70% individuals, 25% schools, 5% commissions from suitable books and courses; no selling user data.

• Economics: near-zero-cost organic acquisition, 3% free-to-paid conversion, about $38 expected lifetime value.

5) Global growth via free channels

• Programmatic SEO: 1,200 public word pages with examples, pronunciation, and a quiz, targeting long-tail phrases like "how to use mitigate in IELTS writing".

• Short video: 3 daily 30-second clips, "a word in a scene," on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, linking to the free word page.

• Study communities: value-first posts on Reddit, Discord, and Telegram, plus a free weekly word pack instead of ads.

• Product Hunt and Show HN launch, with a public roadmap and open-source core to attract contributors.

• Partnerships with IELTS teachers and university clubs: free class pack and 20% recurring commission per subscription.

• Marketing copy in Arabic, Urdu, and Indonesian for first visits, then an English interface.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: "IELTS" is a trademark; add a no-affiliation disclaimer and avoid protected test papers.

• Content: definitions/examples written in-house, CC0 or owned images, no copying existing dictionaries or datasets.

• Privacy: GDPR/COPPA compliance for minors, minimal data, account export/deletion, no voice recordings without consent.

• Technical: TTS errors; human review of top 300 words and recorded fallbacks.

• Technical: AI hallucination; strict rules, report button, human review, usage limits.

• Operational: quality at scale; editorial checklist, native-speaker review of 10% of scenes, archived versions.

• External dependency: LLM/TTS providers may change terms or prices; keep a local rule-based path without external services.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Name: Lexora

Tagline: Learn a word once in context, and it will stay in your memory for a long time.
