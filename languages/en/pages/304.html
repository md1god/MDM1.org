Raseed — Full Project Plan

Describe your internal tools… and build them in minutes.

A complete execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the rollout plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target Audience

• Operations (Ops) teams in small and medium-sized companies that need dashboards and internal forms without hiring a developer.

• Project managers, accountants, and HR officers who manage daily data scattered across separate spreadsheets.

• Small agencies and freelancers who build client delivery tools quickly and at lower cost.

• IT Managers in organizations that want an internally hosted tool with strict security controls.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Conversational visual building: the user writes a description of the tool in simple natural language or Arabic, and the system generates the screens, the logic, and the data connections; it can then be edited with drag-and-drop — faster than traditional visual builders.

• Native Arabic support from the ground up: a full RTL interface, right-aligned layout generation, and Arabic field labels without imposed templates.

• Schema-aware data model: the system automatically recognizes the structure of the connected database or API and suggests the fields and relationships instead of manual wiring of every field.

• Lightweight and fast execution: a lightweight runtime that works on a resource-limited machine, with a free self-hosting option.

• Ready-made domain templates (inventory tracking, HR requests, support tickets) customizable with a single click.

• Payments and billing gateway partnership: every generated application comes ready with exportable financial totals and reports.

3) Scope of the First Release (8 tasks maximum)

1. Drag-and-drop app editor (components: tables, forms, charts, indicators).

2. Conversational app generator: text description → screen structure, fields, and relationships.

3. Integration with PostgreSQL, MySQL, and REST API (three sources minimum).

4. Simple permissions system: roles (admin, editor, viewer) at the application and data level.

5. Ready templates: 3 initial templates, customizable.

6. Cloud-hosted version that works instantly + local execution via Docker.

7. Data export and import (CSV/Excel) and basic reports.

8. Team dashboard: member invitations and activity log.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free to start: 2 active apps, 2 users, 1 data source, offered as a full trial without a credit card.

• Professional: ~$19/month — unlimited apps, 10 members, 5 data sources, 30-day activity log.

• Team: ~$49/month — advanced permissions, additional integrations, backups, priority support.

• Enterprise (Self-hosted): annual license starting at $999 — on-premises hosting, SSO authentication, formal invoicing.

• Additional revenue: paid template marketplace (70/30 revenue share with creators).

5) Global Rollout Plan Using Free Channels

• YouTube and Twitch: short tutorials (2–5 minutes) building a complete tool from scratch, subtitled in Arabic/English/Spanish.

• Reddit and specialized forums: sharing open templates and answering developers' questions about internal tools.

• Arabic communities: Facebook and Telegram groups for entrepreneurs and freelance accountants — weekly live demos.

• Product Hunt and Hacker News: launch of a free trial with an instant try-it link.

• Free template catalog: search-optimized pages for each template (example: "Ready-made inventory tracking system").

• Partnerships with schools and institutes: free educational accounts for universities and business schools.

• Referral program: free credit for every referred user who upgrades to a paid plan.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: compliance with local data protection regulations (GDPR for the European market) when hosting customer data — requires Data Processing Agreements (DPA) and regional hosting options.

• Legal: licensing risks of the open-source components used (commitment to MIT/Apache and LGPL) — legal review before launch.

• Legal: liability for the tools users build (e.g., exposing sensitive data) — clear terms of use and disclaimers.

• Technical: difficulty competing on support for hundreds of sources — the solution: focus on deep quality for three sources first, then expand on demand.
