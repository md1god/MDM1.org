مِعْيَدة — Complete Project Plan

Plan your week by dragging, and get one shopping list sorted by store aisles—even offline.

Plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, revenue, rollout, risks. Under construction; not launched.

1) Target audience

• Families cooking 5–7x/week need a fast evening decision, not ten recipes; planning 45→10 minutes.

• Office workers shopping 1–2x/week forget half the quantities; one aisle-sorted list, not by recipe.

• Shared housing (3–5) cooking "shared meals": scale by headcount in one tap, no manual math.

• Special diets (vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy allergy): drop one ingredient and propagate to all days, not delete a recipe.

• Weak signal/in-store phone users: no account, offline after first load, changes on device.

• Nutrition coaches/dietitians: multi-client plans, PDF/text export for WhatsApp/email.

2) Differentiators (why our product)

• "Recipe as a draggable block": dish stored locally with its data, drag to any of 7 days, copy freely; no external imports or network wait.

• Aggregate before sorting: "onion" in 4 dishes → "onion 3 pieces" once, in vegetables aisle, not random.

• 8 preset aisles (vegetables, protein, dairy, grains, spices, frozen, bakery, other); reorder or add "local market".

• "1 tbsp olive oil" + "30 ml oil" merge as one item; gentle alert on unit conflicts, no wrong number.

• No sign-up, no ad tracking; data in browser via IndexedDB, JSON export and permanent delete anytime.

• Initial load under 150 KB; drag-and-drop under 16 ms, even on a $100 phone.

3) First-release scope (6 tasks max)

• 7-day responsive grid (1 column phone, 7 desktop), touch/mouse drag-and-drop, Ctrl+Z undo.

• 30-dish local library (vegetable rice, grilled chicken, lentil soup...); add via fields: name, ingredients, quantity, aisle, prep time.

• Merge identical ingredients, convert base units (g, ml, piece, spoon), scale servings 1–12 linearly with stated rounding.

• Aisle-sorted list: checkbox per line, "18 items, 7 bought", copy text, tidy A4 print.

• Offline via Service Worker + IndexedDB, "Saved on your device" bar, clear full-storage message.

• Settings: interface language (Arabic/English), aisle order, default serving size, plan export/import.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free: grid, local library, aggregated list, offline use; unlimited dishes/days, no card.

• "Assistant" $3/mo or $24/yr: unlimited named plans, encrypted two-phone sync via user-held key, weekly email reminder.

• "Family" $7/mo up to 5: invite household to edit one plan, auto-split who buys what, no per-person accounts.

• Seasonal libraries, one-time $4: 50 dishes by season/region (e.g. "Ramadan dishes", "street meals"), locally editable after purchase.

• Ethical extra from Somalie: small commission on partner-store "ingredient delivery"; hidden unless explicitly requested.

• No in-app ads, no data selling, no base-tier price rise before 12 months from first launch.

5) Free-channel global rollout plan

• Simplified-Arabic + English landing page; 20-second silent animation: drag a dish, onion unified in list.

• TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 3 cuts: "3 dishes ⇒ one onion", "shopping offline", "7 days in 10 minutes"; 3 posts/week, 8 weeks.

• Reddit (r/MealPrepSunday, r/frugal) + Arabic Facebook groups: tips only, links only if asked, trust before tool.

• 10 food bloggers (5k–50k): free custom dish library named after them in-app; honest review, no forced verdict.

• Static search pages for real questions ("combine onion quantities from recipes?", "shopping list without internet") + Schema.org data, XML sitemaps.

• "Try it now with no sign-up" in every post; track saved weekly plans, not clicks; goal 20k active users in 6 months.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• No copied recipe text; dishes written from scratch with generic ingredients; optional "Source" field filled only by user.

• Register name/logo/shape/colors as new trademark; check local databases before launch for food-sector conflicts.

• Short privacy policy (device-only data), full delete, minors protected by no personal data in free version.

• Unit conflicts (cup vs grams) can yield wrong numbers; per-ingredient conversion lists and "check the quantity" alert, not silent guessing.
