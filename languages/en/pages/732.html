Wasl — Full Project Plan

Sync once, deliver your data to every client and agent instantly.

A complete execution plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, rollout plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

"Wasl" product plan

1) Target audience

• Real-time SaaS app builders: collaboration apps, dashboards, and logistics tools that need instant updates for thousands of users simultaneously.

• AI agent builders: teams building agents that need live data from CRM, stores, and internal systems without rebuilding the integration layer.

• Internal tools teams: field and inventory apps that work offline and sync automatically when connectivity returns.

• Full-stack startups: looking to save about 3 months of work they would have spent building a sync engine from scratch.

• Compliant enterprises: seeking a self-hosted version in the Gulf and Europe to keep data within their borders.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Local-first engine: reads and writes happen on the device with response times under 50 milliseconds, then sync in the background instead of waiting for the server.

• Agent-native interface: an MCP server exposes data as tools for the agent, with permissions and an audit log for every read or write operation.

• Automatic field-level merging: CRDT technology with definable merge policies per field, instead of rigid "last write wins."

• Integration without migration: a CDC connector reads from your current PostgreSQL, Kafka, and webhooks without changing the database or rewriting code.

• Sync diagnostics: a timeline showing every operation, conflict reason, and resolution number within seconds instead of guesswork.

• Instant readiness: an SDK under 100 lines to connect, and a quickstart that works on a new project within 5 minutes.

3) Version one scope (max 8 tasks)

1. Schema definition and a PostgreSQL connector via CDC with schema change detection.

2. CRDT sync engine with field-level merging and a default automation policy.

3. TypeScript and Python clients with full offline cache and WebSocket and SSE subscriptions.

4. MCP server: read, write, and search as tools for the agent with read-only permissions by default.

5. Dashboard: sync timeline, conflict explorer, API keys, and permission policies.

6. Free plan, written documentation, and a quickstart that deploys a live sample within 5 minutes.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Free: one project, 10,000 sync operations per day, two devices, and community-only support.

• Pro at $29/month: 5 projects, 1 million operations per month, 3 devices, alerts, and support within 24 hours.

• Scale at $99/month: unlimited projects, 10 million operations, SSO, and advanced policies.

• Overage: $0.50 per 10,000 operations over the limit, with advance notice at 80% usage.

• On-premises: self-hosted license starting at $499/month for compliant companies.

• 6-month goal: 100 Pro customers, about $2,900/month, with 5% conversion from free users.

5) Global rollout plan using free channels

• GitHub: publish SDKs and open-source examples; goal of 1,000 stars in 3 months with ready templates for common apps.

• SEO and blog: 3 articles per week in English and Arabic targeting terms like "offline first" and "real-time sync."

• Product Hunt and Hacker News: launch with a two-minute live demo; goal of 500 qualified visitors in the first week.

• X, LinkedIn, and Dev.to: 5 posts per week with GIF clips showing two devices syncing; goal of 50,000 impressions per month.

• Reddit and Discord: answer 20 questions per week in React development and AI agent communities, and build an active developer community.

• YouTube: series "Build a sync app in 10 minutes"; goal of 10,000 views in the first month.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Data privacy: GDPR and PDPL compliance through storage localization, permanent deletion on request, and clear processing documentation.

• Open-source licenses: review CRDT and CDC library licenses before integration to avoid transferring legal obligations to users.

• Agent write liability: default read-only permissions and an immutable audit log for every operation.

• Conflict accuracy: property-based tests running millions of operations before every release to measure accuracy rates.

• Schema drift: automatic detection and retry within seconds with notification via email or Slack.

• WebSocket blocking: SSE support and a long-polling alternative so sync works behind firewalls and restricted networks.

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Wasl (وَصْل)

Tagline: Sync once,
