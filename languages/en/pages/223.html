Qidra — Complete Project Plan

Your family's recipes in one pot.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, the audience, the product, the revenue model, the rollout plan, and the risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

1) Target audience

• Families and friends who cook together and share their recipes and shopping lists in real time.

• The immigrant community and those interested in preserving and documenting family recipes.

• Arabic-first users (a fully RTL experience in Arabic and English).

• Self-hosting enthusiasts (Self-hosters) and those concerned with privacy and personal data.

• Small cooking groups, home kitchens, and home food makers.

2) Points of distinction (why choose our product)

• Real-time sync across devices while cooking and shopping (simultaneous edits without friction).

• A shopping list built automatically from the week's recipes and updated in real time while shopping (a dedicated phone mode inside the store).

• Works offline (Offline-first PWA) then syncs when the network returns.

• One-line self-install (Docker) + a free cloud option, with family accounts via invite links.

• Import a recipe from a link or image (light OCR) and convert it into structured, measurable ingredients.

• Privacy by design: no ads, encryption, backup, and your data stays with you in the self-hosted version.

• Real Arabic support: measurements (cup/spoon/tablespoons), temperatures, and RTL without connection loops.

3) Scope of the first version (up to 8 tasks)

1. Recipes core: create/edit/categorize/images/sources with input validation.

2. Real-time sync via CRDT over WebSocket with a revert log (history).

3. A shared shopping list generated from recipes + a completion mode while shopping.

4. An Arabic/English PWA web app that works offline (local sync queue).

5. Family accounts and invite links + role management (editor/viewer).

6. Import a recipe from a link or image (OCR) and convert it into an ingredients structure.

7. A weekly meal plan with drag-and-drop linked to the shopping list.

8. A test suite + CI + self-deployment (Dockerfile and a single run script) and a free cloud version.

4) Pricing and profit model

• Free to start: open-source self-hosting, or free cloud (one family, 200 recipes, 1 GB, basic sync).

• Pro — $5/month or $45/year: unlimited families and devices, unlimited OCR, advanced meal plans, backup, budget spending reports.

• Groups — $15/month: cooking groups and small kitchens (multiple members, permissions, reports).

• Supporting revenue: recipe templates, printable PDF booklets (an elegant legacy edition), later a light link to an ingredients marketplace — without annoying ads.

5) Global rollout plan through free channels

• Developer communities: GitHub (Arabic/English README + Star history), Hacker News (Show HN), Hacker News/self-hosting subreddits, AlternativeTo and the Self-Hosted directory.

• Cooking communities: r/cooking, r/mealprep, family recipe WhatsApp and Telegram groups, family Facebook pages.

• Short content: TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Reels — a series "Turn your grandmother's recipe into a database."

• Launch and events: Product Hunt, Uneed, BetaList, weekly posting on Product Hunt alternatives.

• SEO: comparison articles + landing pages in Arabic and English + static public recipe pages.

• Partnerships: small cooking influencers in exchange for a free Pro account, weekly "share your recipe" contests with symbolic prizes.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Legal: recipes imported from websites or books may be owned — the commitment that the user adds their own recipes, a takedown/DMCA policy, and preventing the saving of copyrighted texts via OCR.

• Legal: data privacy (GDPR, personal data protection laws in the Gulf/EU) — clarifying that the self-hosted version is managed by the user, and a clear data processing policy for the cloud.

• Legal: a trademark for the name and logo in the target markets before expansion, and clear terms of use for free accounts.

• Technical: the difficulty of real-time multi-writer sync (conflicts/lost edits) — solved with CRDT and strict sync tests.

• Technical: the size of images and media in self-hosting — automatic compression and clear limits.

• Technical: relying on a central service for the core feature, which may fail or be imitated — keeping the core open and a fast experience as a line of defense, and flexibility to work offline.

7) Proposed name and marketing tagline

Proposed name: Qidra

Marketing tagline: Your family's recipes in one pot.
