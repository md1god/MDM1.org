Sikka — Complete Project Plan

Log your daily public transit trips, and discover your impact and journey in one place.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, launch plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan for "Sikka" — a public transit trip-tracking service

1) Target audience

• Daily commuters in cities that rely on metro, buses, and trains, especially students and employees.

• People interested in personal statistics: those who want to know distance traveled, most-used lines, and the carbon footprint saved compared to driving.

• Transit communities: those interested in sharing trips, encouraging friends, or keeping a documented personal travel log.

• Intercity travelers who use regional rail networks.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• No complicated account: log a trip in two steps, and it supports anonymous login (device ID only).

• Works without a network: trips are stored locally first, then synced when the connection returns.

• Multiple modes of transport in one standard: metro + bus + train + tram + ferry, with automatic line detection via geolocation.

• Privacy first: precise location is masked before public sharing, with full control over who sees trips.

• Free of annoying ads in the core layer, and open source to build community trust.

• Multilingual with a full Arabic interface from day one (native RTL).

• Lightweight awards and badges that encourage consistency without toxic competition.

3) Scope of the first version (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Manually log a trip (start point + arrival + mode + line).

2. Automatic trip detection via GPS with a review screen.

3. Offline local storage + automatic sync when connected.

4. Trip log and basic statistics (distance, number of trips, mode, estimated carbon saved).

5. A shareable public profile with automatic hiding of precise location.

6. Follow friends and simple interaction (like/comment) with a timeline feed.

7. Initial achievement badges (first trip, 100 km, 7 consecutive days).

8. Privacy and language settings (Arabic/English) inside the app.

Note: everything outside this list is deferred to the second version.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Completely free to start: trip logging, statistics, and the social feature at no cost.

• Optional paid plan "Sikka Pro" at a symbolic monthly price:

• Data export (CSV/JSON) and advanced reports.

• Personal heat maps and line analysis.

• Custom profile badges and a private link.

• Group support (optional donation) via the platform.

• A free public API for personal use, and a paid tier for commercial apps.

• No selling location data to advertisers; revenue relies on subscription and donation rather than targeted ads.

5) Global growth plan through free channels

• Open source on GitHub to attract contributors and translators.

• Public transit communities on Reddit, Telegram, and Discord, hobbyist forums, and rail-watching groups.

• Short content (personal statistics clips, "Trip of the Week") on TikTok/Instagram/YouTube.

• Group challenges such as "A Week Without a Car" to create organic viral spread.

• Student partnerships with universities and environmental clubs as free ambassadors.

• Search engine optimization (SEO) for articles comparing modes of transport and carbon footprint.

• Product Hunt / Hacker News at official launch.

• Crowdsourced translation of the app to open new markets at zero cost.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

Legal:

• Privacy protection (GDPR and the like): collecting location imposes explicit transparency and prior consent.

• Terms of use for public transit schedule data: reliance on official APIs or open sources with a clear license.

• Not breaching railway cybersecurity systems or using protected operational marks without permission.

Technical:

• Location accuracy inside tunnels and metro (GPS loss) → solved by QR scanning of the line and stations.

• Battery consumption during continuous tracking → event-point-based tracking rather than continuous recording.

• Data conflict when syncing from multiple devices → solved by a unique trip ID and careful merging.

• Global expansion and multiple providers → an abstraction layer for schedule APIs.

• Misuse or non-consensual tracking → restrict public sharing and default to "private".

7) Proposed name and tagline

Proposed name: Sikka (سِكّة)

Tagline: Log your daily public transit trips, and discover your impact and journey in one place.
