madar — Full Project Plan

Know your daily mobility habits — and you alone own your data.

Full implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, rollout plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: A personal mobility tracking analytics that respects privacy

1) Target Audience

• Mobile professionals who commute for work/study and want to know their time on the road.

• Accountants and freelancers who need to document their daily mobility (donations, taxes, transportation expenses).

• Families and health-conscious individuals (steps, activity patterns, time spent outdoors).

• Those concerned with economy and health who measure their mobility: tourists, delivery riders, athletes.

2) Differentiators

• On-device data processing and optional encrypted export — no mandatory account, no background tracking.

• Low battery consumption via geofencing and fused location instead of constant GPS.

• Automatic "mental map": familiar areas (home, work, hobbies) with local automatic classification.

• Understandable analytics: peak times, weekly patterns, comparison with previous periods — not just points on a map.

• Easy export (CSV/GeoJSON/PDF report) for professional or personal use.

• Works offline and supports later synchronization.

3) First Version Scope (maximum 8 tasks)

1. Local tracking with two layers: Fused Location Provider + motion sensing to reduce readings.

2. Local database (SQLite/Room with encryption) and background scheduling with WorkManager.

3. Automatic detection of "familiar areas" and merging them without user intervention.

4. Daily/weekly summary screen: distance, travel time, number of visits per area.

5. Privacy mode: stop tracking, delete a period, "secret" mode that prevents recording the current location.

6. Data export and local calculation (simple report for costs/distances).

7. Optional encrypted sync (local account or simple sign-in without ad tracking).

8. Performance testing: battery consumption and classification accuracy across different Android/iOS devices.

4) Pricing and Revenue Model

• Free to start: unlimited tracking + basic analytics + CSV export.

• Monthly/yearly subscription: advanced analytics, PDF reports, family sync for up to 5 devices, ad preferences (with no ads at all).

• One-time purchase: "Professional package" for documenting travel (ready-made reports) and local API programming.

• No advertising model and no data sales — revenue comes only from subscriptions and add-ons.

5) Global Rollout Plan Using Free Channels

• Blog/comparison page: "How to know your daily habits without compromising your privacy" in multiple languages.

• Short videos on YouTube/TikTok/Reels: before/after two weeks of analysis.

• Discord/Reddit community for sharing report templates and use ideas.

• Partnerships with digital privacy pages and personal budgeting apps (mutual mentions).

• Product Hunt + Hacker News page targeting privacy and health audiences.

• App store: optimize interface and description in key languages (Arabic, English, Spanish, French).

• Short weekly newsletter: tips for understanding mobility and protecting data.

6) Risks

• Legal: changes in Android/iOS background policies and location accuracy; location privacy is classified as sensitive data (GDPR/CCPA) — consent and data deletion must be explicitly documented.

• Unclear local laws around storing long-term travel logs; declarations may be required in some countries.

• Technical: battery drain on older devices; classification accuracy in dense areas; data loss during database updates.

• Competition: competing with huge mapping services — we need a clear edge in privacy and understandable analytics.

• Infrastructure: encrypted sync may fail on weak networks; it must be designed to be optional.

7) Proposed Name and Tagline

Proposed name: madar

Tagline: Know your daily mobility habits — and you alone own your data.
