Thermio — Complete Project Plan

Full control of your home climate, locally, with no internet and no account.

A complete implementation plan for a new digital project: the idea, audience, product, revenue model, launch plan, and risks. The project is under construction and has not launched yet.

Product plan: local control of smart climate devices

Core idea: Many cheap air-conditioning, heating, and thermostat devices rely entirely on the manufacturer's cloud, so they stop working when the internet goes down, send user data to remote servers, and stop being supported when the company shuts down. Our product solves this problem with full local control inside the home network, with no cloud intermediary.

1) Target audience

• Homeowners and renters who own cheap smart climate devices and want independence from the cloud.

• Home Assistant users and open-system users seeking privacy and real-time control.

• Engineers and automation enthusiasts (tech-savvy) who reject the restrictions imposed on devices.

• Owners of small facilities, farms, and solar/outdoor power stations where the internet is unstable.

• Developers who want an open API to build custom interfaces and automation.

2) Points of distinction (why choose our product)

• Fully local: does not depend on the manufacturer's cloud, so it works during internet outages and with faster response.

• Privacy by default: no data is sent to any external party.

• Independence from the company's lifespan: devices remain usable even if they are officially discontinued.

• Built from scratch: original protocol and software, no copying or modifying third-party code.

• Unified interface: a single abstraction layer that embraces multiple brands and models under one interface.

• Smart climate logic: schedules, temperature schedules, zones, and an Eco mode that work without internet.

• Free to start: the basic version is fully functional with no registration or account.

3) Scope of the first version (8 tasks maximum)

1. A local network scanner that automatically recognizes compatible devices on the home network.

2. An encrypted local connection layer (local key/token) that creates a direct control channel with the device.

3. The unified device model (one entity per device: on/off, mode, target temperature, fan).

4. A simple local web control panel that runs in the browser on the local network.

5. A climate scheduling and automation engine (weekly schedule, night mode, temperature limits).

6. An open local API (REST + WebSocket) for external automation.

7. Ready integration with common open home platforms via a local channel.

8. A one-click installation package (Docker/standalone package) with initial setup within minutes.

4) Pricing and revenue model

• Always free (free-to-start): all functions of the basic local version, with no account and no device count limits.

• Pro tier (symbolic/annual subscription): syncing settings between multiple homes, energy consumption reports, backups, and instant support.

• Commercial license (Lifetime): for multi-site projects and optional cloud connection (fully optional, not mandatory).

• Additional revenue: supporting donation rewards, partnerships with device manufacturers who want free local support.

• Core principle: no core function is locked behind payment.

5) Global launch plan with free channels

• Publishing open-source software on public hosting platforms to attract contributors and trust.

• Technical articles and reviews on smart home and privacy communities (Reddit, Hacker News).

• Short educational videos on YouTube and open-source video platforms.

• Contributions in communities and specialized forums by answering questions about local control.

• Global documentation in multiple languages and community translation to attract non-English-speaking markets.

• An early adopters (Beta) program and tech influencers passionate about privacy in exchange for free early access.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Technical: the diversity and changing nature of device protocols may break compatibility; the solution: an extensible abstraction layer and automated tests.

• Technical: manufacturers changing things to restrict local control; the solution: monitoring changes, fast updates, and documenting alternatives.

• Technical: local network security; the solution: channel encryption, least-privilege principle, and periodic security audits.

• Legal: avoiding infringement of patents or interface rights; the solution: 100% original development and not using registered trademark names.
