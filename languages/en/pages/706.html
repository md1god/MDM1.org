Farz — Complete Project Plan

Spray only the weeds, not the whole field.

Complete implementation plan for a new digital project: idea, audience, product, business model, go-to-market, and risks. The project is under construction and not yet launched.

Product plan for smart spot spraying

1) Target audience

• Small and medium farmers owning 5–50 hectares who spray the whole field today, especially grain and vegetable growers with high pesticide costs.

• Farmers in arid and semi-arid regions (Morocco, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Australia) where weed control on fallow land is a major cash expense.

• Cooperatives, spray contractors, and agricultural consultants serving dozens of farms needing a measurement and documentation tool for savings.

• Small manufacturers and farm engineers wanting an open platform to integrate into their sprayers instead of building computer vision from scratch.

• Tech-savvy farmers, makers, and agricultural schools willing to self-install a kit at a low price.

2) Differentiators (why choose our product)

• Distinguishes crop from weed, not just "green vs soil", with quick calibration from 30 images of the same field done in 10 minutes without an expert.

• Independent control of each nozzle via 20 Hz PWM, reducing spray liquid by 60–90% in fields with uneven infestation.

• Works locally without internet; farm data stays on device; cloud is optional, not a requirement to operate.

• Maintainable standard hardware: camera, controller board, and valve drivers with available parts, plus software support for at least 5 years.

• Truly free to start: firmware and app are open and free, with no mandatory subscription to run the device.

• Low entry cost: semi-assembled kit at about $150, less than 1% of a modern sprayer's price, paid back within one season.

3) Scope of first version (max 8 tasks)

• Dust- and spray-resistant camera node (IP65) with pulsed LED flash, day and night imaging.

• Controller board with 4 to 8 valve outputs, PWM per output, reverse-current and short-circuit protection.

• Local firmware: weed detection, mapping across the working width, and per-nozzle spray decision.

• Android app for Wi-Fi pairing, showing infestation map and two-step calibration.

• Five ready crop profiles (wheat, corn, tomato, cotton, alfalfa) and a "fallow land" profile for empty fields.

• GPS logging of infestation map and free export as GeoJSON for use in any agricultural software.

• Safety mode that automatically stops spraying on nozzle clog, signal loss, or exceeding set speed.

• Diagnostics panel showing saved-liquid counter and fault log, with optional signed OTA update.

4) Pricing and profit model

• Free plan: app and firmware and five crop profiles and one device, for life and with no fees.

• DIY kit at $149 (camera, board, 4 valves, cables, printed housing) with 15–20% margin.

• Ready assembled version at $399 with 12-month warranty and installation support, 35–45% margin.

• Pro subscription at $7/month or $69/year per farm: unlimited devices, seasonal analytics, maps and prescriptions.

• Service revenue: installation and training at $80 per visit, and a crop-model marketplace where the developer keeps 70% of sales.

• API license for small sprayer manufacturers, annual contract starting at $2000 per model.

5) Global go-to-market via free channels

• GitHub and Hackaday.io: publish firmware and 3D housing for free with a 12-minute build video, and 3 YouTube videos per month.

• Open-source agricultural communities and AgOpenGPS and ROS Agriculture forums: weekly sharing and request for field testing from members.

• WhatsApp, Telegram, and Facebook groups for farmers in Brazil, India, Kenya, and Morocco, with two posts per week in local languages.

• Agricultural extension programs and universities: 10 free field trials in 2026 in exchange for published data and independent review.

• Short content and SEO articles in Arabic, English, and Portuguese on "how much spot spraying saves per hectare", with before/after clips.

• AgriTech competitions and hackathons and local fairs with free booth via partnership with a cooperative or extension agency.

6) Risks (legal and technical)

• Pesticide regulation: some countries require sprayer equipment certification and operator training; start with flexible markets and prepare a compliance file per country before selling.

• Liability for crop damage or spray drift: clear terms of use, liability cap, product insurance, and auditable operational log.

• Design freedom: review patents in spot spraying before shipping, and license software under A
